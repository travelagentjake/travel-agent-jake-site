COSTA DEL SOL, SPAIN

Spain's original package holiday coast, and still one of the easiest, most reliable holidays I book. A short flight into Malaga, warm sunshine for most of the year, and a genuine choice of resort towns, from lively Benalmadena and Fuengirola to smart, glossy Marbella and quieter Nerja, all strung along the same stretch of coastline. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to the Costa del Sol, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about the Costa del Sol

The Costa del Sol gets a bit of a reputation for being the obvious choice, and I think that is exactly why it keeps selling so well. It is a short flight, the weather almost never lets you down, and because it covers so much ground, from the high rise seafront at Benalmadena to the old fishing streets of Marbella and the cliffs above Nerja, there is a genuinely different holiday on offer depending on which bit you pick. It suits first timers who want something easy just as well as it suits clients who have been ten times and know exactly which town they are after.

I compare prices across the suppliers I work with for flights, hotels and transfers to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whatever mix of beach, nightlife and old town charm you are after.

!
Jake's top tip

The Costa del Sol runs year round, not just in summer. It has a proper winter sun season too, so if you want some warmth in January or February without flying long haul, this is one of the first places I point clients towards.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is the classic Costa del Sol holiday, long enough to properly settle into a resort, get a few day trips in and still feel rested. Ten to fourteen nights works well for families who want to really stretch the budget on a longer break, and short breaks of three or four nights are popular too, especially out of season or for a quick dose of winter sun.

It suits pretty much everyone, which is part of why it is booked so often. Families do well in Benalmadena and Fuengirola, where everything from the beach to the restaurants is set up for children. Couples and groups after nightlife and a livelier scene tend to head for Benalmadena, Fuengirola or Torremolinos, while anyone wanting something smarter, with proper restaurants and a bit of glamour, is usually better off in Marbella. Nerja suits clients who want the same sunshine and sea but a slower pace and a prettier, more traditionally Spanish town.

Getting there

Malaga Airport (AGP) sits right on the coast between Torremolinos and Malaga city, and has direct flights from most major UK airports, including both London Gatwick and Heathrow, Manchester, Birmingham, Bristol, Edinburgh, Glasgow and Belfast, with easyJet, Jet2, TUI, British Airways, Ryanair and Vueling all operating routes. Flight time is around two hours forty minutes to three hours, and flights run all year, not just in summer.

Because the airport sits roughly in the middle of the coast, transfer times vary a fair bit depending on which resort you pick. Torremolinos is the closest, around ten minutes away, with Benalmadena around fifteen to twenty minutes and Fuengirola around twenty five to thirty minutes. Marbella is further, usually forty five minutes to an hour, and Nerja, in the opposite direction, is a similar fifty minutes to an hour.

!
Jake's top tip

If you are staying in Marbella or Nerja, I always check whether a private transfer works out better value than a shared coach once you are travelling as a family or a group, since the extra distance from the airport makes a real difference to how long you are sat on a bus.

Jake's top tip

Even in the depths of winter, the Costa del Sol is still getting five or six hours of sunshine a day and daytime highs in the mid teens, which is why so many of my older clients use it for a reliable dose of winter sun rather than flying further afield.

Best time to visit

Late May, June and September are my favourite months to recommend, with genuinely hot, swimmable sea and noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices than peak summer. July and August are the hottest and busiest months, with daytime temperatures regularly into the thirties, so I always book those well ahead for clients who want those specific school holiday dates. What sets the Costa del Sol apart from a lot of other destinations I book is that it does not simply shut down over winter. November through to March stays mild, often in the mid to high teens by day, which makes it one of the most popular winter sun options I offer clients who want some warmth without a long flight.

Four real, bookable picks spread across the coast, from a lively family resort to an adults only boutique base and a smart hotel in the heart of Marbella.

A large hotel, spa and apartment complex in Benalmadena with several pools, regular daytime entertainment through the season, and a short stroll down to the beach and the Puerto Marina. A long standing, dependable choice for families that I book regularly. I can price this one up for you.

An adults recommended boutique hotel in Torremolinos, a short walk from the beach, with a calmer, more polished feel than the busier family resorts nearby and a pool area built for actually relaxing in. The one to pick for clients who want to be properly in among the town itself rather than out on a resort strip. I can price this one up for you.

Getting around

The Costa del Sol has one of the easiest local transport networks of anywhere I book. A coastal train line (the Cercanias) runs between Malaga Airport, Malaga city, Torremolinos, Benalmadena and Fuengirola, so you can hop between those towns cheaply without a car at all. A regular bus network covers Marbella, Nerja and the smaller towns in between. Taxis are plentiful, and hiring a car for a few days is a popular way to see Mijas, Nerja or get up into the hills, especially if your base is Marbella.

Places to visit

Malaga Old Town & the Alcazaba

Picasso was born in Malaga, and the city has leaned into it, with a dedicated Picasso Museum alongside the Moorish Alcazaba fortress, the Roman theatre beneath it, and the Gibralfaro Castle on the hill above for sweeping views over the port. A genuinely good city to pair with a beach based stay.

Marbella Old Town & Puerto Banus

Marbella's Casco Antiguo is a genuine old Andalusian town, all whitewashed walls and orange trees around the Plaza de los Naranjos, while a short drive along the coast brings you to Puerto Banus, the marina famous for its superyachts, designer shops and people watching.

Nerja: the Balcon de Europa & the Caves

A clifftop balcony with some of the best views on the whole coast, in the middle of a genuinely pretty, low rise town that never got the high rise development of its neighbours. Just outside town, the Nerja Caves are a huge network of limestone caverns and one of the most visited sights in the region.

Mijas Pueblo

A whitewashed hill village above Fuengirola, with narrow cobbled streets, donkey taxis, and views right out over the coast. A popular half day trip from any of the main resorts, and a good way to see a more traditional side of Andalusia without travelling far.
