CANADA

Niagara Falls, the old stone streets of Quebec City, turquoise lakes in the Rockies and the neon pull of Toronto. Canada is one of the biggest countries on earth, so here's exactly how I book it, what TUI's real escorted tours cover and what they cost.

What Jake says about Canada

Canada comes up a lot with clients who want a big bucket list trip rather than a straightforward beach week, and TUI is currently the only one of my three main operators, TUI, Jet2holidays and easyJet holidays, that sells it. Neither Jet2holidays nor easyJet holidays flies this far west yet. Even through TUI, Canada isn't sold as a pick your own hotel package, it's a set of real escorted tours, each with its own route, flights, hotels and a long list of included experiences built in.

That actually makes it easier to plan rather than harder. Canada is roughly 40 times the size of the UK, so trying to string together flights, car hire and hotels yourself across provinces and time zones is a serious undertaking. An escorted or self drive tour means the route, accommodation and (on the guided tours) an English speaking guide are already sorted, you just turn up and see the country.

!
Jake's top tip

I always explain upfront that Canada isn't one holiday, it's several completely different ones depending on which coast you head for. The first conversation is always which region and pace suits a client, not which hotel they fancy.

Trip length & who it suits

TUI's Canada tours run anywhere from 8 to 13 nights depending on the route, with trip length and pace varying a lot between them. The eastern city tours are classed as a leisurely to moderate pace with a mix of guided sightseeing and free time, while the self drive tour through the Rockies covers serious distance each day behind the wheel. None of the three tours I've picked out below has an upper age limit, and only the Eastern Explorer has a minimum age, at 10. They suit couples, friends and families with older children who want a proper look at the country's cities, mountains or both, rather than a single resort stay. The self drive route particularly suits confident drivers who are happy covering long distances and want total flexibility over when they stop.

Getting there

TUI flies into Canada from a choice of UK departure airports depending on the tour. The eastern tours fly into Toronto with a wide choice of UK airports including Belfast City, Birmingham, Edinburgh, Glasgow, London City, Gatwick, Heathrow, Manchester and Newcastle, while the Rockies self drive tour flies into Vancouver from Heathrow or Manchester. Flight time is around 8 hours direct to Toronto and a little under 9 hours direct to Vancouver. All flights, transfers and transport between stops on the guided tours, or car hire on the self drive tour, are included in the TUI price and protected under TUI's ATOL licence (ATOL 2524).

On entry requirements, UK passport holders don't need a visa for a short Canadian holiday, but you do currently need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA) to fly in, which is separate from a visa and currently costs CAD$7 (about £3.70). It's applied for online before you travel, through the official Canada.ca website only, and most applications are approved within minutes. I always warn clients to apply directly on the government's own site, since several unofficial lookalike sites charge far more for the same thing.

!
Jake's top tip

Only ever apply for a Canada eTA on the official canada.ca website. I've seen clients get caught out paying two or three times the real fee on copycat sites that look official but aren't linked to the Canadian government at all.

Best time to visit

Each tour only runs across a handful of months, so the best time to go really depends on the route you pick. Canada's Twin Cultures runs year round and departs from around £1352pp. Canada's Great Parks of the West Self Drive Tour only runs May to September, since the high mountain passes aren't reliably open outside summer, from around £1912pp in May up to around £2146pp in June and July. Canada's Eastern Explorer also only runs May to September, from around £2745pp in May up to around £2880pp in June. Late September and early October bring Canada's famous autumn colour, which I always flag to clients who ask specifically about the leaves, though it falls right at the end of the self drive tour's season and outside the eastern tours' current departure windows.

!
Jake's top tip

June tends to be the best balance of good weather and value across most of the tours. If a client's heart is set on seeing autumn colour, I talk them through what's actually bookable rather than promising a date none of the tours currently cover.

Three ways to see Canada

Canada is a natural multi centre destination, since even a single TUI tour strings several cities, parks or provinces together in one booking. Here are three real, bookable tours from TUI's current range, so you can see which route actually fits what you want to see.

Canada's Twin Cultures

8 nights. Toronto, Niagara Falls, Quebec City and Montreal, mixing English and French Canadian culture in one trip. Leisurely pace, 4 included experiences and a half day Niagara Falls trip. From £1352pp, flying from a wide choice of UK airports. No minimum age.

Canada's Great Parks of the West Self Drive Tour

13 nights. Vancouver, the Okanagan Valley, Glacier and Banff National Parks, the Icefields Parkway, Jasper and Whistler. Self drive with car hire included, no guide needed. From £1912pp, flying from Heathrow or Manchester. Runs May to September only.

Canada's Eastern Explorer

11 nights. Toronto, the Thousand Islands, Montreal, Quebec City, Ottawa, Algonquin Park and Niagara Falls. Moderate pace, 12 included experiences and almost all meals covered. From £2745pp, flying from a wide choice of UK airports. Minimum age 10.

Prices shown are the lowest advertised per person fare found for each tour across its current departure months, correct at the time of writing and subject to change. All three are ATOL protected under TUI's licence (ATOL 2524).

Where to stay

Because these are escorted or self drive tours rather than pick your own hotel holidays, TUI chooses the hotels for location rather than naming them in advance, the same as its Japan and China tours. What TUI does confirm is the standard. Canada's Twin Cultures splits its 8 nights between three hotels of a 3 star, 3.5 star or 4 star standard across Toronto, Quebec City and Montreal. Canada's Great Parks of the West Self Drive Tour uses eight hotels of a 3 star and 3.5 star standard along its route from Vancouver to Whistler. Canada's Eastern Explorer spreads its 11 nights across six hotels of a 3 star and 4 star standard, covering Toronto, Montreal, Quebec City and Ottawa. I'll confirm the exact named hotels with you once a specific departure date is booked.

Getting around

On the two guided tours, every transfer, scenic train leg and piece of transport between stops is arranged and included as part of the TUI package, with a local English speaking guide throughout on the Twin Cultures and Eastern Explorer routes, so there's nothing to organise yourself once you've booked. The Great Parks of the West tour works differently, since it's a self drive itinerary with car hire included in the price rather than a guide. You'll need a full UK driving licence and be at least 21, with a surcharge for drivers under 25, and you'll be covering genuine distance each day along mountain roads like the Icefields Parkway, so it suits confident, experienced drivers rather than a first time visitor who'd rather not drive on holiday.

!
Jake's top tip

If driving yourself through the Rockies doesn't appeal, Canada's Twin Cultures or Eastern Explorer give you the same scale of scenery and city sightseeing with a guide doing all the driving and planning instead.

Places to visit

The CN Tower, Toronto

One of the tallest freestanding structures in the world, with a glass floor and views over Lake Ontario on a clear day. The obvious first stop on every eastern tour, and a genuine highlight even for clients who aren't keen on heights.

Niagara Falls

Included on both eastern tours, with the option to get right up close on a boat trip or walk behind the curtain of water itself. One of those sights that's genuinely bigger and louder in person than any photo suggests.

Banff National Park & the Icefields Parkway

Turquoise glacial lakes, snow capped peaks and one of the most scenic drives on earth, all covered on the Great Parks of the West self drive route. Lake Louise alone is worth the entire trip for a lot of clients.

Old Quebec, Quebec City

A UNESCO listed old town with cobbled streets, a clifftop hotel and genuinely French Canadian atmosphere, included on both the Twin Cultures and Eastern Explorer tours. It feels more like a corner of France than anywhere else in North America.
