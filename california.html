CALIFORNIA

Hollywood and the Strip, Yosemite and Big Sur, the Golden Gate Bridge and the desert, all on one trip. Here's exactly how I book California, the three real TUI tours that cover it, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about California

California is one of the most requested trips I get and also one of the most misunderstood, because there isn't currently a flight and hotel package to Los Angeles or San Francisco on any of my three main operators, TUI, Jet2holidays and easyJet holidays. Neither Jet2holidays nor easyJet holidays flies long haul at all at the moment, and TUI, the only one of the three that does, doesn't currently sell a standalone California city break either. What TUI does sell is three genuinely good escorted and self drive tours, all starting and finishing in Los Angeles, that between them cover the whole state and more.

That isn't a compromise. California is enormous, Los Angeles to San Francisco alone is further than London to Edinburgh twice over, and a tour with flights, transfers, accommodation and a hire car or a guide already sorted is honestly the easier way to see it properly rather than trying to piece together three or four separate city stays yourself.

!
Jake's top tip

The first thing I ask a client who wants California is whether they want a guided coach tour, a self drive road trip, or the full two week version with San Diego and the national parks thrown in. All three are real, bookable TUI tours, not something I've had to improvise.

Trip length & who it suits

The shortest of the three tours runs 8 nights, the other two run 14, so California isn't really a one week destination once you factor in the distances involved. The 8 night escorted tour suits first time visitors who want the big three, Los Angeles, Las Vegas and San Francisco, covered properly without driving themselves. The 14 night self drive suits independent, confident drivers who want to set their own pace on the Pacific Coast Highway. The 14 night escorted Western Discovery Tour suits clients who want the full sweep, San Diego, the Grand Canyon, Zion and Bryce Canyon included, without picking up a hire car at all.

Getting there

All three tours fly into Los Angeles International Airport and include international flights, taxes and transfers in the price, flying from London Heathrow as standard with alternative UK departure airports available on an indirect basis. Flight time to Los Angeles from Heathrow runs around 11 hours direct. UK passport holders don't need a visa for a short US holiday, but do need to apply for an ESTA before flying, which I always point clients towards on the official US government site only. My full ESTA fee guide has the current costs and how to apply safely.

!
Jake's top tip

Always apply for an ESTA through the official US government site, esta.cbp.dhs.gov, or the official mobile app. I've seen clients overcharged on copycat sites that have nothing to do with the US government.

Best time to visit

Spring, from March to May, and autumn, from September to October, give the best balance of warm weather, lower prices and comfortable desert temperatures for the Death Valley and Grand Canyon legs. Summer, June to August, is still popular and the surest bet for consistently dry weather in San Francisco, but it's also when Death Valley and Palm Springs become genuinely dangerous to be outdoors in for long, so I always flag that to clients on the self drive and Western Discovery tours. Winter is mild in Los Angeles and San Diego but wetter, and this is when San Francisco sees the most rain.

!
Jake's top tip

If a client is set on the self drive tour, I steer them towards spring or autumn departures rather than summer. Death Valley in July regularly tops 45 degrees, and that's not a pleasant place to be stuck with a flat tyre.

Where to stay

Because all three options are tours rather than a flight and hotel package, TUI picks hotels for location and standard rather than naming them in advance, the same as its Japan and Canada tours. What TUI does confirm is the overall standard. Western Panorama splits its 8 nights across 6 hotels of a 4 star and 3 star standard. The Best of the West Self Drive spreads its 14 nights across 11 hotels of a 2, 3 and 4 star standard on a self catering basis, since it's a self drive route with no tour guide. The Western Discovery Tour splits its 14 nights across 11 hotels of a 4 star standard and 3 hotels of a 3 star standard, with breakfast included on 3 mornings. I'll confirm the exact named hotels with you once a specific departure date is booked.

!
Jake's top tip

None of the three tours name hotels upfront, TUI confirms them closer to departure once the exact route for your dates is finalised. I always flag that honestly rather than guessing a specific hotel.

Getting around

On Western Panorama and the Western Discovery Tour, coach transport and an English speaking guide are included throughout, so there's nothing to arrange yourself. On the Best of the West Self Drive, a hire car is included in the price, a Volkswagen Jetta or similar from Alamo, picked up at Los Angeles International Airport. You'll need a full UK driving licence and be at least 21, with a surcharge for drivers aged 21 to 24, since you'll be covering real distance most days. If you add pre or post tour nights in Los Angeles yourself, the city is very spread out and really needs a car or ride share app to get around comfortably, it isn't a walkable city in the way London or even Las Vegas is.

On the tours themselves, free time in San Francisco usually means a cable car ride, Fisherman's Wharf, or a trip out to Alcatraz, while a free day on the Strip in Las Vegas works exactly as it does on my standalone Las Vegas guide. In Los Angeles itself, the Hollywood sign, Griffith Observatory and Rodeo Drive are easy additions either side of your tour dates.

Places to visit

Griffith Observatory, Los Angeles

Free to enter, with some of the best views in the city over Los Angeles and the Hollywood sign, especially at sunset. A popular add on for anyone spending extra nights in LA either side of their tour.

Golden Gate Bridge, San Francisco

Covered on all three tours' free time in San Francisco, walkable across or viewable from several spots around the bay, including Crissy Field and the Marin Headlands on the far side.

Venice Beach & Santa Monica Pier

The classic LA boardwalk scene, street performers, muscle beach and the Santa Monica Pier Ferris wheel right next door. Easiest reached with extra nights tacked on to either end of a tour.

San Diego

Covered on the Western Discovery Tour, a relaxed coastal city with a famous zoo, Old Town and the USS Midway Museum, and a noticeably different, more laid back pace than LA just up the coast.
