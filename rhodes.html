RHODES, GREECE

The largest inhabited medieval town in Europe, a whitewashed hilltop village with its own ancient Acropolis, and some of the most reliable sunshine in the Mediterranean. Rhodes is one of those destinations that quietly does everything well, history, beaches, nightlife and food, which is exactly why I get asked about it so often. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Rhodes, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Rhodes

Rhodes Old Town is a genuine UNESCO World Heritage Site, a walled medieval city built by the Knights of St John that you can still wander around for hours without seeing the same corner twice. Outside the walls, the island has one of the longest, most reliable summer seasons in Greece, with a mix of lively resorts, quiet family beaches and the whitewashed hilltop village of Lindos all within an hour or so of each other.

I compare prices across the suppliers I work with for flights, hotels and transfers to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whether that is a straightforward week in a beach resort or a longer stay built around the Old Town and Lindos.

!
Jake's top tip

Rhodes has one of the longest seasons of any Greek island, with flights running from April right through to early November. If you want warm, reliable weather without the August crowds and prices, I always point clients towards late May, June or September first.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is the classic Rhodes holiday, long enough for a few days by the pool, a full day in the Old Town and another exploring Lindos, without feeling rushed. Ten to fourteen nights works well if you want to properly split your time between a resort beach and a base in or near Rhodes Town.

It suits families, couples and groups who want a genuine mix of beach time and sightseeing, history lovers, and anyone who wants reliable sunshine with real character rather than a purely purpose built resort strip. It is less suited to clients who want total peace and quiet everywhere they look, since the busier resorts like Faliraki have a lively, younger nightlife scene, although plenty of quieter spots exist on the same island if that is what you are after.

Getting there

Rhodes International Airport (Diagoras) has direct flights from a wide range of UK airports, including London Gatwick, Heathrow, Luton and Stansted, Manchester, Birmingham, Bristol and East Midlands, with easyJet, Jet2, TUI, British Airways and Ryanair all operating routes in season. Flight time is around four hours direct. Flights run from roughly April through to early November, with far fewer options over winter.

The airport sits close to Ixia and Rhodes Town, with most resorts on the west and east coasts between around twenty minutes and an hour and a half away by transfer.

!
Jake's top tip

If your hotel is on the south east coast around Lindos, Kiotari or Gennadi, factor in a longer transfer of up to ninety minutes. I always set expectations on transfer times before booking so nobody is caught out on arrival.

The sea stays warm enough to swim comfortably right through October and into November, so a late September or early October trip gives you proper summer warmth with noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices than peak season.

Best time to visit

Late May, June and September are my favourite months to recommend, with warm, swimmable sea, reliably sunny weather and noticeably thinner crowds than peak summer. July and August are the hottest and busiest months, with temperatures regularly into the low thirties, so I always book those well ahead if clients want those specific dates and suggest building in plenty of pool or beach time in the middle of the day. October still offers a genuinely warm, sunny shoulder season with lower prices, and Rhodes keeps a reasonable amount going through the winter too, since it is one of the larger, more established Greek islands.

Four real, bookable picks around Rhodes, from a five star spa resort to a boutique base in Rhodes Town, a family all inclusive and a value self catering option.

Best five star spa escape, Ixia

Atrium Platinum Luxury Resort & Spa

A five star resort on Rhodes' north west coast at Ixia, around five kilometres from Rhodes Town, with three outdoor pools and an indoor pool, some rooms with their own private plunge pool, and a spa with sauna, whirlpool and massage treatments. All inclusive plus, half board and bed and breakfast options are all available. I can price this one up for you.

Best boutique base, Rhodes Town

Rodos Park Suites & Spa

A smart hotel set in its own gardens in Rhodes Town, under a kilometre from the resort centre and a short walk from the Old Town, with an outdoor pool and a wellness spa offering sauna, steam room and massage treatments. Half board and bed and breakfast options available. The one to pick for clients who want to be on foot for the sights. I can price this one up for you.

Best family all inclusive, Kolymbia

Leonardo Kolymbia Resort

A large all inclusive resort on the east coast at Kolymbia, around 250 metres from the beach, with two outdoor pools including a children's pool, three restaurants, a spa and regular daytime entertainment through the season. A dependable, well liked choice for families. I can price this one up for you.

Best value, Pefkos

Pefkos Beach Hotel

A family run hillside hotel above Pefkos, around 600 metres from the beach, with three outdoor pools including a children's section and a choice of bed and breakfast or self catering studios and apartments with their own kitchen facilities. Good value with lovely sea views, though it is a walk uphill from the drop off point so I would steer clients with limited mobility elsewhere. I can price this one up for you.

Getting around

Rhodes Old Town itself is largely pedestrian, with cobbled lanes best explored on foot. A regular local bus network, split into two companies covering the east and west coasts, connects Rhodes Town with the main resorts, and is a cheap way to get around if you do not mind a bit of waiting. Taxis are widely available, and hiring a car for a day or two is a popular way to see Lindos, the Butterfly Valley and Prasonisi in your own time. Organised coach excursions cover all the main sights too, which I can book as part of your trip.

Places to visit

Rhodes Old Town & the Street of the Knights

The largest inhabited medieval town in Europe and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with the cobbled Street of the Knights leading up to the Palace of the Grand Master, built by the Knights Hospitaller in the fourteenth century. Wander the walls, browse the old bazaar and stop for dinner in one of the countless tavernas tucked into the side streets.

Lindos & the Acropolis of Lindos

A whitewashed hillside village about an hour from Rhodes Town, crowned by an ancient Acropolis with a Temple of Athena Lindia and sweeping views down over St Paul's Bay. The walk up is steep, but donkey rides are available for anyone who would rather not climb, and the village itself is full of narrow lanes, boutique shops and sea view cafes.

Seven Springs (Epta Piges)

A shaded woodland spot inland from Kolymbia, where seven natural springs feed a cool stream. You can walk through a narrow, pitch dark tunnel that the water runs through to reach a small lake, or take the longer path around above ground if you would rather stay dry. A lovely, cooler break from the beach on a hot day.

Prasonisi

The sandy spit at the island's southern tip, where the Aegean and Mediterranean seas meet, is one of Europe's best known windsurfing and kitesurfing spots thanks to the strong, reliable summer winds. Non watersports fans still enjoy the drive down and the dramatic, wilder scenery compared with the busier north of the island.
