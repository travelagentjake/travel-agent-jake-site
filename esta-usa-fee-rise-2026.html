ESTA FOR THE USA: THE FEE HAS GONE UP AGAIN, HERE'S HOW TO APPLY PROPERLY

If you're a UK passport holder heading to the States, you'll almost certainly need an ESTA, and the official fee has just gone up for the second time this year, to $40.62 from 16 October 2026. That's still only around £30, but websites that mimic the real government site have been charging UK and Irish travellers several times that for exactly the same form. Here's what an ESTA actually costs right now, who genuinely needs one, and the one rule that stops you overpaying for something that should be straightforward.

The short version

An ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorisation) is the US government's advance authorisation for visa free visits of up to 90 days, business or tourism, for UK and other eligible passport holders arriving by air or sea. It's not a visa, there's no interview, and most applications are approved within minutes.

The genuine fee for an approved application is $40.62 (roughly £30 at today's exchange rate) from 16 October 2026, up from $40.27 earlier this year and just $21 before 2025. Once approved, it's valid for 2 years or until your passport expires, whichever comes first, covering unlimited trips in that time. The only place to apply is the official government site, esta.cbp.dhs.gov, or its official mobile app. Anywhere else charging you for an ESTA is a third party add on at best.

Why the fee keeps rising

For years, an ESTA cost a flat $21. That changed in 2025, when a US federal law (the budget reconciliation act known as HR-1) introduced new mandatory fee components for ESTA and several other immigration related charges, pushing the total to around $40. Since then, the fee has been adjusted annually for inflation under that same law, which is why it's moved twice in 2026 alone.

Who actually needs one, and what it covers

  • 1UK passport holders visiting for up to 90 days, for business or tourism, arriving by plane or cruise ship, including a brief stop or connecting flight through the US. You don't need one if you already hold a valid US visa.
  • 2It's valid for 2 years or until your passport expires, whichever comes first, and covers as many separate trips as you like within that time. It can't be renewed. Once it expires, you simply apply again.
  • 3If you renew your passport after getting an ESTA, you'll need a new application linked to the new passport number, even if the old ESTA hadn't expired yet.
  • 4Every traveller needs their own approved ESTA, including children and infants, each linked to their own passport.
  • 5A valid ESTA doesn't guarantee entry. It means you're authorised to travel to the US and request entry, the final decision is still made by a border officer when you arrive.

Jake's top tip

If you're heading to the US via Canada or Mexico by land, ESTA isn't the right form at all, that route uses a separate I-94 process. ESTA is specifically for arriving by air or sea.

The one rule that stops you overpaying

Apply only through esta.cbp.dhs.gov or the official ESTA mobile app. That's it, that's the whole rule, and it's worth repeating because it's the single most common way UK travellers end up paying far more than they should for a form that genuinely costs about £30.

Lookalike websites that mimic the official government page are a well documented problem, not a rare scam. US Customs and Border Protection has publicly warned that these sites don't work on behalf of the government, aren't guaranteed to protect your data properly, and that money paid to them can't be refunded by the real authorities if something goes wrong. One widely reported case saw travellers charged as much as $150, around £112, more than three times the genuine fee, once a lookalike site's own "processing fee" was added on top. An Irish MEP who looked into it found some sites were charging roughly ten times the real price.

A simple way to check you're in the right place: the real site's address ends in .gov, it's run by US Customs and Border Protection, and it will never ask you to pay more than the current official fee shown above.

Before you apply: the photo requirement, timing, and what gets you refused

  • 1You'll now need to upload a photo of your own face as part of the application, alongside your passport details. The UK government's own current guidance notes that if your ESTA account doesn't have this photograph on file, you may need to apply again before you travel, so it's worth checking your existing ESTA account if you got one before this became standard.
  • 2Apply at least 72 hours before you fly, officially, though plenty of applications are approved within minutes. The safest habit is applying as soon as you book, rather than leaving it until the week before.
  • 3Common reasons an ESTA gets refused: a previous overstay or breach of US immigration rules, a criminal record, a previous deportation or removal from the US, false or inconsistent answers on the form, or having been in Iran, Iraq, Libya, North Korea, Somalia, Sudan, Syria or Yemen on or after March 2011, or in Cuba on or after 12 January 2021.
  • 4There's no appeal within the ESTA system itself. If you're refused, the next step is applying for a full visa through a US embassy or consulate, which involves booking an interview well ahead of travel, so it's not something to leave until close to your departure date.
!
Jake's top tip

Fill the form in properly rather than quickly. Minor inconsistencies between your passport details and what you type in, even innocent typos, are a genuine cause of unnecessary refusals.

Quick answers

How much does an ESTA cost for US travel in 2026?

The official fee for an approved application is $40.62 (around £30) from 16 October 2026, up from $40.27 earlier in the year and just $21 before 2025. A denied application costs $10.62. Always pay through the official esta.cbp.dhs.gov website or app, never a third party site.

How long does an ESTA last once it's approved?

Two years from approval, or until your passport expires, whichever comes first, covering unlimited trips to the US of up to 90 days each for business or tourism. If you renew your passport in that time, your existing ESTA no longer applies and you'll need to apply again under the new passport.

Why do some websites charge much more than $40.62 for an ESTA?

Third party websites that mimic the official US government site have been known to charge well over the real fee, in one widely reported case as much as $150, more than three times the genuine cost, plus their own added processing fees. US Customs and Border Protection has repeatedly warned travellers to apply only through the official esta.cbp.dhs.gov website or app, which is the only place the real fee applies.

What's the new photo requirement for ESTA?

As part of tightening identity checks, applicants now need to upload a photo of their own face alongside their passport details. The UK government's own guidance notes that if your existing ESTA account doesn't have this photograph on file, you may need to apply again before you travel.
