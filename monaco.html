MONACO

Monte Carlo's casino, a harbour full of superyachts and some of the priciest real estate on earth, squeezed into just over two square kilometres on the French Riviera. Here's exactly how I book Monaco, the hotels I can book for you, and what it actually costs to visit.

What Jake says about Monaco

Monaco punches well above its size. It's the world's second smallest country, you could walk from one end to the other in under an hour, but it packs in a world famous casino, a royal palace on its own rocky headland, Michelin starred restaurants and a harbour lined with yachts that cost more than most houses. Clients either want it for a special occasion, an anniversary, a proposal, a milestone birthday, or they want it bolted onto a wider French Riviera trip alongside Nice, Cannes or Eze.

Monte Carlo works well as a proper package holiday, flights and a named hotel booked together, flying into Nice Cote d'Azur airport just along the coast. easyJet also flies direct to Nice from several UK airports, so Monaco works well as a tailor made trip booked around those flights too.

!
Jake's top tip

Monaco genuinely suits two to four nights on its own, but most of my clients get more out of the trip by pairing it with a few nights somewhere else on the Riviera. I've set out three real ways to do that further down this guide.

Trip length & who it suits

Two to four nights is plenty for Monaco itself. It suits couples, honeymooners and anyone celebrating a special occasion who wants glamour rather than a sprawling resort, plus Formula 1 fans who want to see the famous street circuit in person. It's less suited to families on a tight budget or anyone wanting a classic beach week. Monaco's only real beach, Larvotto, is small, partly manmade and nothing like the long sandy stretches of Spain or the Greek islands, and prices across the board, hotels, restaurants, even a coffee, run well above the European average.

Getting there

Monaco has no airport of its own, so every flight lands at Nice Cote d'Azur airport, about 20 miles along the coast. Flights from the UK take around 2 hours 10 minutes from Gatwick, Heathrow, Luton, Manchester and several other airports, with easyJet, British Airways and Jet2 all operating the route. From Nice airport it's a transfer of 30 to 45 minutes into Monaco, by taxi in around 30 minutes, by the number 110 airport express bus in around 40 minutes, or by train from Nice Saint Augustin station, close to the airport, to Monte Carlo station in roughly 25 to 30 minutes. A transfer is included as part of a package.

UK passport holders don't need a visa for Monaco. It isn't an EU member and isn't formally part of Schengen, but it has an open border with France and follows the same entry rules in practice, so the 90 day in any 180 day limit applies, counted together with any time spent elsewhere in Schengen. The EU's Entry/Exit System, which records fingerprints and a photo on first entry, has been fully operational since 10 April 2026. Your passport needs to have been issued within the last 10 years and have at least 3 months left on it from your planned departure date from the Schengen area.

!
Jake's top tip

I always tell clients there's no border check travelling between Nice and Monaco itself, you'll barely notice crossing from France into Monaco on the train or by road. The passport rules only really come into play at the airport when you first land in France.

Best time to visit

April to June and September to October give the best balance of warm, reliable weather and slightly calmer streets and prices. July and August are hottest and busiest, when the harbour fills with visiting superyachts for the season. The last weekend in May or first weekend in June, when the Monaco Grand Prix takes over the streets, is a brilliant spectacle but hotel prices spike hard and rooms sell out months ahead, so it needs booking well in advance if that's the plan. Winter, December to February, is quieter and noticeably cheaper, with Monaco's harbourside Christmas lights adding some festive sparkle to an otherwise low key season.

!
Jake's top tip

The 2027 Monaco Grand Prix runs Thursday 3 June to Sunday 6 June. If a client wants to go for the race itself, I tell them to book as early as possible, hotel rates across the whole principality jump significantly that weekend.

Where to stay in Monaco

Monaco is so compact that almost every hotel sits within walking distance of the casino, the harbour and the Japanese Garden, so location matters less than it does elsewhere. These four are all bookable as a flight and hotel package.

Novotel Monte Carlo

4 star. The most accessible of the four on price, around 260m from the train station and 600m from the harbour, with an outdoor seasonal pool, a gym with sauna and an a la carte restaurant. A sensible, well located base without the price tag of Monaco's grander names. I can price this one up as a package.

Port Palace

4 star. A smaller, more boutique feeling hotel overlooking Port Hercule and the Prince's Palace, just 400m from the harbour. 50 rooms with a spa offering a whirlpool, sauna, steam room and Turkish bath. A good pick for couples who want character over size. I can price this one up for you.

Fairmont Monte Carlo

4 star. Famous for its views over the Mediterranean and straight onto the Monaco Grand Prix circuit's hairpin bend. Three restaurants, a champagne bar, an outdoor rooftop pool and Nikki Beach Club, plus a spa and casino on site. The one to pick for F1 fans or anyone wanting a real Monaco occasion.

Hotel Metropole Monte Carlo

5 star. Monaco's top tier package hotel, right by Place du Casino, with two a la carte restaurants including the Michelin starred YOSHI, a heated saltwater pool, a full spa and a children's club running through summer. The one for a genuine once in a lifetime stay.

Star ratings and facilities as published at the time of writing. I always check current pricing and availability before booking.

Three ways to combine Monaco with the French Riviera

Monaco pairs brilliantly with the rest of the Riviera, and I rarely book it as a trip entirely on its own. Here are three real ways clients do it, each built around a real hotel.

Base yourself in Nice

Stay at the AC Hotel by Marriott Nice, a 4 star beachfront hotel on the Promenade des Anglais with a rooftop pool, and take the coastal train into Monaco, around 25 to 30 minutes each way, for a day or two. Nice gives you more restaurants, nightlife and a proper city beach to come back to each evening.

Split stay: Monaco & Cannes

A couple of nights at the Novotel or Port Palace in Monaco, then on to the 5 star Hotel Barriere Le Majestic Cannes overlooking La Croisette for the rest of the trip. Two very different kinds of Riviera glamour in one booking, best suited to a special occasion with a bit more budget behind it.

A quieter base: Villefranche & Beaulieu

The 3 star Ibis Styles Beaulieu sur Mer, breakfast included and 450m from the beach, sits on the same coastal train line as Monaco and Nice, both under 15 minutes away. A calmer, prettier and noticeably cheaper base with exactly the same easy access to everything.

All three hotels named here are bookable as a package at the time of writing, each as its own separate booking.

Getting around

Monaco is small enough to walk almost everywhere, though it's built on a steep hillside, so expect some genuine climbs between the harbour, the casino district and Monaco Ville on the rock. Monaco makes clever use of public lifts and escalators tucked into the hillside to help with exactly that, worth using rather than always taking the stairs. The local CAM bus network covers the rest, and taxis are plentiful but not cheap. For day trips, the SNCF coastal train line runs along the whole Riviera, so Nice, Beaulieu sur Mer, Villefranche sur Mer and Eze are all a short, simple hop away without needing a hire car.

Places to visit

The Prince's Palace

The official residence of the ruling Grimaldi family, with a Changing of the Guard performed daily at 11.55am on the Place du Palais. The State Apartments open to visitors for part of the year, check ahead since exact dates vary.

Casino de Monte-Carlo

The Belle Epoque casino that put Monaco on the map. Entry to the gaming rooms is around £16.60pp, over 18s only with a passport or photo ID, and a smart dress code applies, no shorts, sportswear or trainers.

Oceanographic Museum

A dramatic cliff edge aquarium founded by Prince Albert I, with a shark lagoon and a rooftop terrace looking straight out over the Mediterranean. Adult entry is around £17pp.

Eze village

A medieval hilltop village a short bus or taxi ride from Monaco, all narrow cobbled lanes and sweeping coastal views, with the Jardin Exotique d'Eze at the very top. One of the easiest, prettiest half day trips on the whole Riviera.
