SWITZERLAND: THE GLACIER EXPRESS & THE SWISS ALPS

One of the most famous train journeys in the world, the Matterhorn towering over a car free mountain village, and a slower, more scenic way to see the Alps than flying between cities. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Zermatt and the Glacier Express, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Switzerland and the Glacier Express

Switzerland is not a destination any of the big package operators sell as a straightforward flight and hotel deal, so I build it as a proper tailor made trip, putting together rail tickets, transfers and named hotels across the suppliers I work with rather than a single off the shelf package. Zermatt is where most of my clients start and finish, a car free village sitting right under the Matterhorn, and from there the Glacier Express is the single thing most people ask me about by name, a slow, panoramic train that crosses the Alps between Zermatt and St Moritz in around eight hours.

I compare prices and routing across the suppliers I work with to find the best fit for your dates, whether that is a few nights in Zermatt alone or a proper multi centre trip riding the Glacier Express end to end.

!
Jake's top tip

A seat reservation is compulsory on the Glacier Express for every passenger, even if you are travelling on a rail pass. I always book this alongside your hotels so it is one less thing to think about, and window seats on the right side of the train get snapped up early in summer.

Trip length & who it suits

Zermatt alone is worth three to four nights, long enough for the cable car up to Matterhorn Glacier Paradise, a day on the Gornergrat railway, and time to just walk around the village. Most of my clients build a longer trip around it, seven to ten nights, pairing Zermatt with a few nights at the other end of the Glacier Express or at another Swiss base. It suits couples, photographers and anyone who has the Glacier Express or the Matterhorn on a bucket list, hikers in the summer months, and skiers and snowboarders in winter, since Zermatt is a genuine four season resort.

It is less suited to clients on a tight budget, Switzerland is consistently one of the most expensive countries in Europe, and it is not a beach holiday, so it suits a very different sort of client to most of what I book.

Getting there

Most of my clients fly into Zurich, which has the best choice of UK departure airports and takes around two hours in the air. From Zurich Airport's own railway station, a direct InterCity train runs to Visp in about two hours fifteen minutes, where you change onto the narrow gauge Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn for the final hour or so up into the mountains to Täsch. Geneva is a useful alternative if it suits your flight times better, with a slightly longer rail journey of around three hours forty five minutes to Zermatt.

Zermatt itself has been car free since 1961, so the railway actually terminates just outside the village at Täsch, where a shuttle train runs every twenty minutes or so for the final twelve minute hop into Zermatt station. Electric taxis and hotel shuttles meet you there for the last short stretch to your hotel.

!
Jake's top tip

The connection at Visp is genuinely seamless, a single change across the same platform with a short window to make it. I always build in a sensible connection time and flag it clearly on your itinerary so there is no need to rush with luggage.

!
Jake's top tip

The Glacier Express does not run at all for a few weeks each autumn while the line is maintained, usually from around 11 October to early December. If riding it is the whole point of your trip, I always check the current year's exact dates before booking anything either side of that gap.

Best time to visit

Switzerland genuinely runs on two separate seasons. Winter, roughly December to April, is ski season, with Zermatt's slopes open year round on the glacier above the village and at their best from January to March. Summer, May to October, is when the Glacier Express runs its full timetable with panoramic observation coaches, and when the hiking trails around Zermatt properly open up, June to September is the reliable window with July and August the warmest and busiest. I generally point clients wanting the Glacier Express itself towards June or September, good weather with noticeably fewer crowds than the August peak.

!
Jake's top tip

If skiing is not the priority, avoid the gap between the ski season ending in April and the Glacier Express's summer timetable starting in early May, a lot of mountain lifts and some hotels close for a few weeks either side of that changeover.

Where to stay in Zermatt

Four real, bookable picks in the village.

Best value

Hotel National Zermatt

A traditional Alpine style hotel close to Bahnhofstrasse, Zermatt's main street, with easy walking access to the shops, restaurants and the Gornergrat railway station. A straightforward, well located base for a first trip. I can price this one up for you.

Best boutique & design

CERVO Mountain Resort

A 5 star chalet style boutique resort set above the village with uninterrupted Matterhorn views, a spa, and its own restaurants. A quieter, design led alternative to staying right in the centre. I can price this one up for you.

Best classic luxury

Grand Hotel Zermatterhof

One of Zermatt's oldest and most storied grand hotels, right in the village centre, with a full spa, fine dining and the kind of old school Alpine glamour clients picture when they imagine a Swiss ski hotel. I can price this one up for you.

Best for families & spa

Le Mirabeau Hotel & Spa

A 4 star family run hotel with its own spa and a central location, also sold by UK ski specialists, so it is a name clients sometimes already know. A comfortable, relaxed base rather than an ultra formal one. I can price this one up for you.

Facilities as published at the time of writing. I always check current pricing, board basis and availability before booking.

Getting around

Zermatt has been car free since 1961, so the whole village is walkable, with electric taxis and hotel shuttles covering the short stretch from the railway station if you are carrying luggage. The Gornergrat railway and the Matterhorn Glacier Paradise cable car both leave from stations in the village and are the two main ways up into the mountains above. Outside Zermatt, Switzerland's rail network is excellent and punctual, and a Swiss Travel Pass covering trains, buses and boats is worth considering if your trip includes more than one Swiss base.

Places to visit

Matterhorn Glacier Paradise

A cable car journey to Europe's highest mountain station at 3,883 metres, with an observation platform, an ice palace carved into the glacier, and views across the French, Italian and Swiss Alps.

Gornergrat

A cogwheel railway climbing to 3,089 metres for one of the best panoramic views of the Matterhorn and the surrounding glaciers, with a self guided exhibition included on the ticket.

The Landwasser Viaduct

A six arch limestone viaduct on the Glacier Express route between Schmitten and Filisur, around 65 metres high, and one of the most photographed moments of the whole journey.

Bahnhofstrasse & the old village

Zermatt's traffic free main street, lined with shops, restaurants and traditional dark timber barns on stone staddle stones, some of them centuries old, right alongside the mountain backdrop.

Multi centre: combining Zermatt with the rest of Switzerland

Because so many of my clients come to Switzerland specifically for the Glacier Express, I usually build this as a genuine multi centre trip rather than a single base. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

Zermatt & St Moritz

The classic option, riding the Glacier Express the full eight hours end to end, then finishing with a few nights in the glamorous lakeside resort of St Moritz, home to some of Switzerland's grandest historic hotels.

Zermatt & Lucerne

Mountain time in Zermatt followed by a classic Swiss lake city break at the Art Deco Hotel Montana, overlooking Lake Lucerne and the famous medieval Chapel Bridge, an easy rail journey from Visp.

Zermatt & Interlaken

For clients who want the Jungfrau region as well as the Matterhorn, finish the trip at the Victoria-Jungfrau Grand Hotel & Spa in Interlaken, a member of the Leading Hotels of the World, within easy reach of the Jungfraujoch excursion.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.

Cost of living: what things actually cost

Switzerland uses the Swiss franc, not the euro, and it is consistently one of the most expensive countries in Europe. Prices below are shown in pounds with the franc equivalent alongside, based on averaged data for Zurich and a rate of roughly £1 to CHF 1.10. Jake's top tip

Switzerland is expensive everywhere, but Zermatt itself is on the pricier end even by Swiss standards. I always set client expectations on spending money honestly before they travel, especially for food and drink on the mountain restaurants.
