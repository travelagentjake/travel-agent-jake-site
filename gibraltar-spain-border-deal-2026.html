GIBRALTAR'S BORDER WITH SPAIN HAS CHANGED: WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEANS FOR YOUR HOLIDAY

In July 2026 the border fence between Gibraltar and Spain came down for the first time in decades, part of a new UK and EU treaty that brings Gibraltar's entry rules broadly into line with the Schengen area. If you're holidaying on the Costa del Sol and fancy a day trip, flying straight into Gibraltar, or just want to know whether it changes anything for your passport, here's exactly what's changed and what hasn't.

Three things worth knowing before you go

The short version is that checking hasn't disappeared, it's just moved.

Land border

Routine checks are gone

The fence came down on 15 July 2026. Walking or driving between Spain and Gibraltar no longer means a passport check or a queue at the frontier itself.

Airport and port

Checks moved here instead

Fly or arrive by sea and you'll clear Gibraltar's own immigration first, then a separate Schengen check carried out by Spanish officials, with EES biometrics for most UK visitors.

Still applies

The 90/180 day rule

Gibraltar isn't in the EU or technically part of Schengen, but it now applies Schengen's entry conditions, and time spent there counts towards your 90 day allowance.

What actually changed, and why

Gibraltar's status had been unresolved since the UK left the EU in 2020, with the border effectively frozen while a deal was negotiated. That deal, a treaty between the UK and the EU covering Gibraltar, took provisional effect on 15 July 2026. The physical fence at the La Línea crossing came down overnight, and routine immigration and customs checks on people crossing the land border on foot or by car were removed with it. Gibraltar's own government describes the change in its published treaty guidance as removing every physical barrier related to the movement of people between the EU and Gibraltar.

Gibraltar hasn't actually joined the EU or the Schengen area itself. What's changed is that its short stay entry conditions for visitors now formally align with Schengen's own rules, and the checking of those rules has moved away from the land border entirely, to the airport and the port instead.

Jake's top tip

If you've crossed into Gibraltar before and remember a British style passport booth at the frontier gate, that's gone. Still carry your passport with you while you're there, since you may be asked for it at the airport, the port, or in a spot check.

Passport checks and EES: what actually happens now

Arrive by air or by sea and you'll go through two separate checks in sequence. Gibraltar's own Borders and Coastguard Agency checks you in first, and Spanish officials then carry out a Schengen check immediately after, to make sure the external border of the Schengen area is being properly controlled. A joint facility is being built at Gibraltar Airport specifically to handle both checks in one place. Leaving Gibraltar, the order is reversed, Spanish officials check you out of Schengen first, then Gibraltar processes your exit.

For most UK visitors, that Schengen check now includes the EU's Entry/Exit System, EES, the same digital system that replaced manual passport stamping across the rest of the Schengen area. The first time you're registered it records your fingerprints and a facial image, after that it's a quick scan rather than a full registration. Gibraltar residents are exempt from EES regardless of their nationality, but a UK holidaymaker who doesn't live there isn't.

Your passport still needs to meet the same rule as anywhere else in Schengen, issued within the previous 10 years, and valid for at least 3 months after the day you leave Gibraltar or the wider Schengen area. I've written separately about that 10 year passport rule if you want the full detail.

ETIAS, the pre travel authorisation that's been expected across the whole Schengen area for a while now, still isn't live. It's been delayed repeatedly and is currently expected sometime in 2027 at the earliest, so there's nothing extra to apply for before a Gibraltar trip right now. That will change once it finally launches.

!
Jake's top tip

Officials can still ask for proof you're a genuine visitor, a return ticket, your hotel booking, travel insurance, or evidence of funds. Keep digital or printed copies handy rather than assuming you won't be asked.

Day trips from the Costa del Sol, and flying straight in

A lot of my clients staying around Marbella, Estepona, Fuengirola or Sotogrande fancy a day trip across to Gibraltar, and this is genuinely the part that's got easier. Cross on foot or in a hire car and there's no passport check and no routine queue at the land crossing itself anymore. The crossing is open 24 hours a day, and like most borders it still has busier and quieter times, generally quieter in the late morning and early afternoon, and busier around the Spanish workforce's commuting hours and on Spanish public holidays.

A hire car crosses with no extra paperwork created by the treaty itself, just the usual rental agreement and driving documents you'd carry on any Spanish holiday. Walking across is often quicker than driving when traffic backs up, since pedestrians use a separate lane from vehicles.

You don't need to be staying in Spain at all to visit. easyJet flies direct to Gibraltar from several UK airports, including Gatwick, Birmingham, Bristol, Edinburgh and Glasgow. Arrive this way and you'll go through the same dual check described above, Gibraltar's own immigration first, then the Schengen check with EES.

Shopping and duty free: it's not quite what it was

Gibraltar's reputation as a VAT free shopping stop has taken a real turn under the new treaty. A temporary allowances regime lets visitors bring goods from Gibraltar into Spain tax free up to €300 (around £250 at current exchange rates) if you're crossing by land, or €430 (around £355 at current exchange rates) if you're arriving by sea or air, according to Gibraltar's own government technical notice. Alcohol, tobacco and fuel have their own separate limits on top of that, though the exact figures hadn't been published in detail at the time of writing, so check with HM Customs Gibraltar directly before you rely on bringing back a specific amount.

Gibraltar itself is also phasing in a new domestic transaction tax of 15%, expected to rise to 17%, on retail sales, replacing part of its old tax free status. That's made traditional duty free pricing, especially on tobacco and alcohol, less of a guaranteed bargain than it used to be. None of that's stopped demand though. Gibraltar's government reported euro sales at some businesses up by more than 240% in the fortnight after the fence came down, according to Euro Weekly News, largely driven by day trippers and cross border shoppers.

!
Jake's top tip

Don't plan a big shopping trip to Gibraltar purely on the assumption of old style duty free prices. Check specific items you're after before you go, the gap to Spanish high street prices isn't as wide or as predictable as it was.

Quick answers

Do I still need to show my passport to cross into Gibraltar from Spain?

Not for a routine crossing on foot or by car at the land border, since those checks were removed when the fence came down on 15 July 2026. You'll still need your passport, and may be asked for it, if you arrive by air or sea, or in a spot check.

Does time spent in Gibraltar count towards my Schengen 90 day allowance?

Yes. Gibraltar now applies Schengen's entry conditions, so days spent there count towards the same 90 days in any 180 day period as time spent in Spain, France or anywhere else in the Schengen area.

Do I need ETIAS to visit Gibraltar?

Not yet. ETIAS still isn't live anywhere in the Schengen area, it's been delayed repeatedly and is currently expected in 2027 at the earliest.

Is Gibraltar still worth visiting for duty free shopping?

It's more mixed than it used to be. A new allowances regime and a domestic transaction tax are both being phased in, so check specific prices rather than assuming a blanket VAT free bargain.

Can I still fly direct to Gibraltar from the UK?

Yes. easyJet flies direct from several UK airports, including Gatwick, Birmingham, Bristol, Edinburgh and Glasgow.
