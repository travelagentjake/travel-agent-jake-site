GALAPAGOS ISLANDS

Giant tortoises that have walked the same lava trails for a century, sea lions that nap on the pavement outside the harbour cafes, and blue footed boobies doing a mating dance that genuinely looks like a party trick. The Galapagos is the one trip that comes up again and again with my clients who grew up watching nature documentaries, and it is booked completely differently to a normal holiday. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to the Galapagos, the real cruises and lodges I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about the Galapagos

The Galapagos is not a flight and hotel holiday, it is an expedition, and that changes almost everything about how it gets booked. There is no resort to pick and no beach to simply flop on for a fortnight. Almost every visit is built around either a small ship cruise between the islands or a few nights at a land based lodge with daily boat excursions, because the National Park strictly controls where and how visitors can move around to protect the wildlife.

I compare the real cruises and lodge programmes on sale across the operators I work with to find the ship, route and style that actually suits your dates, budget and sea legs, since every trip here is sold as its own named departure rather than a generic package.

!
Jake's top tip

The first question I ask a new Galapagos client is how they feel about several days living aboard a small ship. Some people love it, others would much rather sleep in the same bed every night and do day trips instead. Both are genuinely available, and it is the first thing that decides which style of trip suits you.

Trip length & who it suits

Most clients travel for 9 to 12 nights in total, made up of a night or two in Quito either side of the trip plus 3 to 8 nights actually in the islands, whether that is aboard a cruise or at a land based lodge. Clients combining the Galapagos with mainland Ecuador, the Amazon or Peru, which I cover further down, tend to book 14 nights or more.

It suits clients who want a genuine wildlife bucket list trip, anyone happy with early starts for excursions, a few days afloat or at least on the water daily, and travellers who are not precious about having the same room every single night. It is less suited to anyone who gets seasick easily and wants to avoid a boat entirely, which is where the land based lodge option below tends to work far better, or to clients wanting a relaxed beach holiday, since this is a trip built around structured daily excursions rather than lounging by a pool. I am always upfront about that before anyone gets too attached to a particular idea.

Getting there

There are no direct flights from the UK to Ecuador. Most routes connect through Madrid with Iberia, Amsterdam with KLM, or through a US gateway such as Miami or Houston with American or other carriers, for a total journey time of around 17 to 20 hours door to door to Quito, the usual gateway city. From Quito, a further internal flight of around two hours gets you to Baltra or San Cristobal in the Galapagos itself, with a short stop in Guayaquil on some routings.

Every visitor also needs to buy a Transit Control Card before checking in for the flight to the islands, and pay the Galapagos National Park entrance fee in cash on arrival, both of which I cover under practical info further down.

!
Jake's top tip

I always build a night in Quito either side of the Galapagos leg into the itinerary rather than a same day connection, both to let clients recover from the long flight from the UK and as a buffer against any delay on the short hop out to the islands.

Weather by month

The Galapagos sits on the equator but the Humboldt Current keeps it far cooler and drier than most people expect. Roughly speaking there are two seasons, a warmer, wetter run from December to May and a cooler, mistier run from June to November known locally as the garua.

!
Jake's top tip

Pack layers whatever month you travel. Days feel properly warm in the sun, but the breeze on an open boat and the cooler garua mornings from June to November catch a lot of first time visitors out.

Best time to visit

December to May, the warmer season, suits families, swimmers and snorkellers best, with calmer seas, warmer water and short sharp showers rather than all day rain. June to November, the cooler garua season, tends to suit divers and keen wildlife watchers more, since the colder water brings in bigger marine life such as whale sharks and penguins, even though mornings are mistier and crossings between islands can be choppier. For value, May, September, October and November tend to be the quietest and cheapest months to travel, while June to August and the fortnight either side of Christmas and New Year are the busiest and priciest, so I always suggest booking those well ahead.

Three ways to see the Galapagos

The Galapagos does not come as one simple flight and hotel package, every trip is either a named small ship cruise or a land based lodge programme, so the real choice is which style suits you. Here are three genuine, bookable options to show how different they can be.

Classic cruise, In Darwin's Footsteps

9 days door to door, 2 nights in Quito then 6 nights aboard the Santa Cruz II with Metropolitan Touring, a 90 guest small ship. The eastern route takes in Baltra, Espanola, Seymour, San Cristobal, Santa Cruz, Santa Fe and South Plaza, with a glass bottom boat, kayaks and two outdoor hot tubs on board. From £5,875pp.

Ultra modern, Celebrity Flora

7 nights aboard the Celebrity Flora, a purpose built 100 guest mega yacht for the Galapagos, sailing the Inner Loop with naturalist led excursions, kayaking, snorkelling and the option to camp on deck under the stars one night. Most clients add 2 nights in Quito either side. From £6,404pp.

Land based, Pikaia Lodge

4 days, 3 nights at this lodge on Santa Cruz Island, extendable up to 7 nights, with full board, daily yacht excursions to sites such as North Seymour and Bartolome, snorkelling gear, mountain bikes and VIP airport lounge access included. The one to pick if a multi night cruise is not for you. From around £3,540pp.

Prices shown are the lowest advertised per person fare found for each option at the time of writing, based on two sharing, and are subject to change, availability and exchange rates. I always check the live price and cabin or room availability before quoting.

Getting around

Independent travel around the Galapagos is not really a thing. National Park rules mean almost every site can only be visited with a certified naturalist guide, as part of a cruise itinerary or a lodge's own excursion programme, usually travelling between sites by small boat or Zodiac style dinghy. Budget travellers island hopping under their own steam can use the basic inter island speedboats that run between the handful of inhabited islands such as Santa Cruz, San Cristobal and Isabela, a bumpy couple of hours each way, though this limits you to day trips from a land base rather than visiting the more remote, uninhabited islands most people picture. In Quito itself, taxis and ride hailing apps are cheap and plentiful, and I arrange private airport transfers as standard either side of the Galapagos leg.

Things to do

Most of the Galapagos itself is only visited as part of a cruise or lodge excursion, so the bookable tours and tickets worth having on the radar are mainly in Quito, where every trip spends a night or two.

Places to visit

North Seymour & Mosquera Islet

A short crossing from Baltra brings huge colonies of frigatebirds and blue footed boobies nesting right beside the trail, plus a sandy islet where sea lions and their pups bask just metres from the water's edge. A gentle, easy walking site often used early in a cruise.

Santa Cruz Highlands & Charles Darwin Research Station

Puerto Ayora, the islands' main town, is the gateway to the highlands where giant tortoises roam genuinely wild, and to the Charles Darwin Research Station's breeding programme for the species. The best spot to see tortoises outside a zoo enclosure anywhere on earth.

Espanola Island

Home to the entire world's waved albatross colony between roughly April and December, plus a dramatic blow hole sending seawater shooting through a lava tube, and some of the most colourful marine iguanas in the archipelago. One of the furthest sites from the central islands, usually visited on longer cruises.

Bartolome Island

A short, steep climb up a wooden staircase to the viewpoint beside Pinnacle Rock gives the single most photographed view in the whole archipelago, a volcanic landscape that looks almost lunar, with Galapagos penguins sometimes spotted in the bay below.

Multi centre: combining the Galapagos with somewhere else

The Galapagos does not come as one simple flight and hotel package, so I build it as a tailor made trip from the outset, and that makes it straightforward to extend. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

Galapagos & Quito's Old Town, Cotopaxi & Otavalo

Extend the mainland stay either side of the islands with a proper look around Quito's UNESCO listed Old Town, a day trip to Cotopaxi National Park for one of the world's highest active volcanoes, and the famous Saturday market at Otavalo for textiles and crafts.

Galapagos & the Ecuadorian Amazon

Fly from Quito to Coca, then take a motorised canoe down the Napo River to a lodge such as Sacha Lodge, set within its own private rainforest reserve, for guided canopy walks, piranha fishing and night time wildlife spotting on top of the islands themselves.

Galapagos & Peru's Machu Picchu

Fly on from Quito or Guayaquil to Lima, then up to Cusco for the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu itself, a combination several of the cruise operators I work with already sell as a single joined up trip. I cover Peru in full in my separate Peru guide.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.

Jake's top tip

Since Ecuador uses the US Dollar, there is no local currency to buy or sell, which makes budgeting genuinely easy. Small denomination notes and coins are handy for tips and small vendors outside the main tourist areas.

Quick practical info

The essentials, at a glance.

CurrencyUS Dollar, Ecuador's official currency, cards widely accepted in Quito and on board ships, small notes useful for tips and local vendors
Plug typeType A and B, the same as the US, voltage 120V, a UK adaptor and a voltage converter for UK appliances are both needed
LanguageSpanish, with English spoken by most guides on cruises, lodges and in Quito's tourist areas
Flight time from the UKNo direct flights. Around 17 to 20 hours total to Quito via Madrid, Amsterdam or a US gateway, plus a further internal flight of around 2 hours on to the islands
Time differenceMainland Ecuador is 5 hours behind the UK, 6 hours behind during British Summer Time. The Galapagos Islands themselves run a further hour behind the mainland
Entry requirementsUK passport holders do not need a visa for tourist stays of up to 90 days in Ecuador, and tourists can stay in the Galapagos itself for up to 60 days. Your passport should be valid for at least 6 months beyond your arrival date, and travel insurance is mandatory for every visitor to the islands
Galapagos specific feesA Transit Control Card, bought before checking in for your flight to the islands, equivalent to around £15 at the time of writing, and a National Park entrance fee paid in cash on arrival at the Galapagos airport, equivalent to around £151 for adults and £76 for under 12s. Both fees are fixed in US Dollars by the Galapagos authorities. A Biosafety Sworn Declaration must also be completed online 48 to 72 hours before you fly out there

Fees and requirements change from time to time, I always check the current amounts and process with clients before they travel.
