NORWAY: FJORDS & NORTHERN LIGHTS

Bergen's colourful harbour and fjords, Tromso's Arctic nights and a genuine shot at the Northern Lights, three real multi centre options, weather by month and what it actually costs, everything you need to plan a trip to Norway.

What Jake says about Norway

Norway is really two completely different holidays wearing the same passport stamp. Bergen is a short, pretty city break built around a UNESCO listed wharf and a day out on the fjords, the sort of trip that suits a long weekend any time from spring to autumn. Tromso, 1,000 miles further north and well inside the Arctic Circle, is a dark, cold, genuinely bucket list break built almost entirely around chasing the Northern Lights. Clients ask me for one or the other far more often than both, so the first question I ask is simply which holiday they actually want.

Bergen and Tromso can both be booked as a flight and hotel package, and where a package is not the right shape for the trip I build it from separate flights and hotels instead.

!
Jake's top tip

The single biggest thing I set expectations on with Norway is the weather and the odds. Bergen is one of the wettest cities in Europe whatever month you go, and the Northern Lights are never guaranteed on any one trip to Tromso, only more or less likely depending on when you go. I always build that into the conversation before anyone books.

Trip length & who it suits

Bergen suits a short break of three or four nights, flights, a hotel and usually a half day fjord cruise or rail trip built around a long weekend. It works well for couples, first time visitors to Norway and anyone who wants a scenic, walkable city without a long flight. Tromso also suits three or four nights, built around nightly Northern Lights chases rather than a single big day out, and suits clients treating it as a genuine bucket list trip rather than a relaxing holiday. Combine the two and you are looking at six to eight nights total, which suits clients who want to see both faces of Norway in one trip and are happy to fly twice to do it.

Getting there

Bergen is an easy, short flight, around 1 hour 55 minutes direct from London Gatwick, with direct flights from a range of UK airports including Gatwick, Manchester, Bristol, Newcastle and Leeds Bradford. Tromso is a longer hop at around 3 hours 15 minutes, and there are now direct seasonal flights from Bristol, Gatwick and Manchester, usually from November through the winter. Outside that direct season, or from an airport that is not on the direct list, Tromso is reached with a connection, most commonly through Oslo on Norwegian or SAS.

!
Jake's top tip

The direct Tromso flights are seasonal and route specific, so I always double check which UK airports are actually flying direct for a client's travel dates before promising a non stop option. A one stop route through Oslo is just as reliable and often has far more flexibility on dates.

!
Jake's top tip

Tromso's polar night, when the sun never rises above the horizon, usually runs from late November to mid January. It sounds bleak but it's actually the best stretch for aurora hunting, since the sky barely brightens all day.

Best time to visit

For Bergen, April to September covers the brightest, driest and warmest months, with June to August the most reliable for long daylight hours even if rain is never fully off the table. For Tromso, the Northern Lights season runs roughly September to March, with the long, dark nights of December to February generally rated the best odds, weather and solar activity allowing. There is never a guarantee on any single trip, since it comes down to clear skies and the aurora actually being active that night. September and March are useful shoulder months for Tromso, pairing a reasonable aurora chance with more daylight to see the city and get out on the water.

Where to stay

Bergen

Almost everyone stays in or around the Bryggen wharf area, the colourful old UNESCO listed waterfront that is also Bergen's main sightseeing draw. It puts you within walking distance of the fish market, the Floyen funicular and the harbour where the fjord cruises depart, so there is little reason to base yourself anywhere else for a short break.

Tromso

The waterfront around the harbour and Storgata is the obvious base, close to the Arctic Cathedral, the Polar Museum and the cable car up Storsteinen. Nearly all Northern Lights tours collect from hotels in this central area, since the whole point is to drive out of the town's own light pollution.

!
Jake's top tip

Facilities and board basis vary a lot by hotel in both cities, so I always confirm the live details for a specific hotel before a client books.

Getting around

Both cities are compact and genuinely walkable. Bergen's Floyen funicular runs right from the city centre up to a viewpoint over the fjords, and the harbour where fjord cruises and day trips depart is a short walk from most central hotels. Tromso's own cable car, Fjellheisen, climbs Mount Storsteinen for a view back over the city, and most Northern Lights tours and whale watching trips collect guests directly from their hotel by minibus or coach, so a hire car is rarely necessary for either leg of the trip.

Three ways to see Norway

Norway rarely gets booked as one single holiday, since Bergen and Tromso are such different trips. These are the three ways I actually book it.

Bergen only, the fjords and harbour break

Three or four nights, built around the colourful Bryggen wharf and a half day Mostraumen fjord cruise, or a full day out on the Flam Railway and Naeroyfjord. The easiest, shortest option and the one I book most often for first time visitors and couples.

Tromso only, the Northern Lights break

Three or four nights, built around nightly aurora chases out of the city's light pollution, with a whale watching trip or dog sledging added depending on the season. The one I book for clients treating this as a real bucket list trip.

Bergen & Tromso combined

Six to eight nights total, flying into Bergen first for the fjords, then an internal flight, usually around two hours and often via Oslo with Norwegian, SAS or Widerøe, up to Tromso for the Arctic leg. This combination doesn't come as a single package, so I build it as two separate flight and hotel bookings priced up as one trip.

As a rough guide, a three night Bergen city break typically starts from around £500 to £750pp including flights, and a three to four night Tromso break, where flights are scarcer and winter demand is high, typically starts from around £700 to £1,100pp. Both move around a great deal with dates, departure airport and hotel, so always confirm current pricing with Jake.

!
Jake's top tip

I always ask whether a client wants the easy short break or the full Arctic adventure before suggesting the combined trip. Doing both in one go is a brilliant holiday, but it is a longer, pricier trip than most people picture when they first say 'Norway'.

Places to visit

Bryggen Wharf

Bergen's UNESCO World Heritage listed row of colourful wooden merchant houses, dating back to the Hanseatic League, and the city's single most photographed spot. Right in the heart of the centre.

Mount Floyen

A short funicular ride from the city centre up to a viewpoint over Bergen's harbour and the seven surrounding hills, with walking trails continuing on from the top for anyone wanting to stretch their legs further.

The Flam Railway & Naeroyfjord

A full day trip from Bergen combining one of the world's steepest railway journeys with a cruise along the narrow, UNESCO listed Naeroyfjord, often sold as the Norway in a Nutshell route. A proper way to see the fjords beyond Bergen's own harbour.

The Arctic Cathedral

Tromso's striking, triangular glass fronted church, one of the city's best known landmarks and a short walk or bus ride from the centre across the Tromso Bridge.

Whale watching

Orcas and humpback whales gather in the fjords around Tromso to feed on herring, roughly October to January, making a whale safari a genuine highlight alongside the Northern Lights during the darker months.

Fjellheisen cable car

Tromso's own cable car climbs Mount Storsteinen for a sweeping view back over the city and surrounding islands, and doubles as one of the easiest spots in town to watch for the aurora on a clear night.

Norway is consistently one of the more expensive countries in Europe to eat and drink out in, worth factoring into a client's spending money.
