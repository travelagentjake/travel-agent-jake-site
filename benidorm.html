BENIDORM, SPAIN

Benidorm gets written off by people who have never actually been, and every time a client lands there for the first time they tell me the same thing, it is so much better than they expected. Two long sandy beaches, a genuinely pretty old town on a headland between them, some of the shortest flight and transfer times of any package destination I book, and a choice of hotels that runs from lively high rise towers to quiet adults only escapes. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Benidorm, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Benidorm

Benidorm's reputation was built decades ago and a lot of people still picture it as nothing but high rises and English breakfasts. The skyline is real, and I will not pretend otherwise, but the old town on the headland between Levante and Poniente beaches is full of narrow lanes, tapas bars and sea view terraces, and the clifftop Balcon del Mediterraneo viewpoint is one of the prettiest spots on this whole stretch of coast. It is also, genuinely, one of the most flexible destinations I book, a short flight, a short transfer, and hotels and nightlife to suit almost anyone, from stag and hen groups to young families to couples who just want a quiet pool and a decent dinner.

I compare prices across the suppliers I work with for flights, hotels and transfers to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whether that is a big lively resort near the Rincon de Loix nightlife strip, a quieter family hotel on Poniente beach, or an adults only base right in the centre.

Jake's top tip

Benidorm splits fairly cleanly into Levante beach, livelier and busier with the famous high rises, and Poniente beach, a little quieter and more residential with a longer promenade. I always ask which kind of trip a client wants before I start looking at hotels, since it changes my shortlist completely.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is the most commonly booked length, long enough for plenty of beach time, a day in the old town and a trip out to Guadalest or Altea, without feeling rushed. Because flights and transfers are both so short, Benidorm is also one of the destinations I book most often as a three or four night city break, for a hen or stag weekend or just a quick dose of sunshine, and at the other end a good number of clients book ten nights, two weeks or longer, including a fair few retired clients who take three or four week winter sun breaks over the quieter months.

It genuinely suits a wide range of clients. Groups of friends book the livelier hotels around Rincon de Loix and the Levante strip for the nightlife. Families do well on Poniente beach or in the many hotels built with kids' pools and clubs in mind. Couples looking for a quieter, adults only trip have more choice here than in most Spanish resorts, with several dedicated adults only hotels right in the town centre. I would not point a client who wants total isolation and empty beaches towards Benidorm, it is a busy, built up resort by design, but almost everyone else finds a version of it that suits them.

Getting there

Alicante Airport, code ALC, is the gateway for Benidorm and one of the most convenient airports I book anywhere, with direct flights from a huge range of UK airports all year round rather than only in summer. Flight time runs at around two to two and a half hours depending on which UK airport you fly from. The transfer from Alicante Airport to Benidorm itself is short too, usually somewhere around forty five minutes to an hour by road, which is noticeably quicker than most of the other Spanish and Balearic resorts I book.

Because Alicante Airport runs flights year round rather than seasonally, Benidorm is also one of the easier destinations to book as a genuine winter sun trip, not just a summer one.

Jake's top tip

That short, year round flight and transfer combination is exactly why I book so many last minute and short break trips to Benidorm. If a client wants sunshine with as little travel hassle as possible, this is usually one of the first places I suggest.

Jake's top tip

September tends to bring the wettest spell of the year to this stretch of coast, usually as short, heavy downpours rather than days of grey skies, so I would not let it put anyone off booking that month, the sea is still at its warmest and the crowds have thinned out from August.

Best time to visit

July and August are the hottest and busiest months, with daytime highs regularly hitting thirty degrees and the beaches, pools and old town all at their liveliest, so I book those dates well ahead for clients who want them. June and September give you almost the same warmth and a properly warm sea with noticeably thinner crowds and better prices, and this is the window I point most clients towards if they are flexible. Benidorm's year round flights also make it a genuine option for winter sun, with mild mid to high teens by day through autumn and into spring, which is exactly why I book so many short winter breaks and longer stays here for clients who just want some sunshine without a long haul flight.

Where to stay

Five real, bookable picks around Benidorm, from a landmark high rise to a quiet adults only base and a luxury resort just inland.

Benidorm landmark, Poniente Beach

Gran Hotel Bali

Benidorm's famous high rise, often described as one of the tallest hotels in Europe, set around 400 metres back from Poniente beach with three outdoor pools, a spa, five bars and a 43rd floor viewing platform. Full board, half board, bed and breakfast and room only options are all available, and it is genuinely popular with both couples and families. I can price this one up for you.

Best beachfront, Poniente Beach

H10 Porto Poniente

A smart four star superior hotel right on Poniente beach, with an outdoor pool, a wellness area and the longer, quieter promenade on this side of town on your doorstep. A good pick for couples and families who want a genuine beachfront stay without being in the busiest part of the resort. I can price this one up for you.

Best adults only, town centre

Hotel Benidorm Centre

A four star adults only hotel right in the centre of town, an easy walk from both Levante beach and the old town, with a rooftop pool and sun terrace. The pick for couples who want to be in the middle of everything without any of the family resort noise. I can price this one up for you.

Best all inclusive family suites, Rincon de Loix

Sandos Benidorm Suites

An all inclusive suites resort in the Rincon de Loix area, a short walk or bus ride from Levante beach, built around family friendly suites with separate living areas, several pools and regular daytime and evening entertainment. A dependable, well liked choice for families who want everything included. I can price this one up for you.

Best luxury escape, a short drive from Benidorm

Asia Gardens Hotel & Thai Spa, a Royal Hideaway Hotel

A five star Thai themed resort set in its own tropical gardens a short drive inland from Benidorm, with an adults recommended wing, an extensive spa and several pools among the palms. For clients who want Benidorm's convenience with a genuinely five star, resort style stay rather than a beachfront tower, this is my go to suggestion. I can price this one up for you.

Getting around

Benidorm itself is compact and very walkable, with both Levante and Poniente promenades easy to stroll end to end and the old town sitting right between them. A cheap, regular local bus network covers the whole resort and runs out to nearby towns like Altea and Villajoyosa, and taxis are plentiful if you would rather not wait. Hiring a car for a day or two is a popular way to see Guadalest, Altea and the Algar Waterfalls at your own pace, and a direct bus service also runs between Alicante Airport and Benidorm for anyone who does not want a private transfer.

Places to visit

The Old Town & the Balcon del Mediterraneo

Benidorm's original fishing village, now a warren of narrow lanes, tapas bars and small shops on the headland between Levante and Poniente beaches. At its tip sits the Balcon del Mediterraneo, a clifftop viewpoint with sweeping views along both beaches, especially lovely at sunset.

Altea

A whitewashed old town around twenty minutes up the coast from Benidorm, built up a hillside to a blue domed church with genuinely lovely sea views. A favourite for a quieter half day out, with plenty of small galleries, cafes and restaurants to wander between.

Guadalest & the Algar Waterfalls

Guadalest is a dramatic clifftop village built around a ruined castle around forty minutes inland, one of the most photographed spots in the whole region. Many day trips combine it with the Algar Waterfalls nearby, a series of natural pools and falls where you can swim in cool mountain water on a hot day.

A day trip to Tabarca Island

Spain's only inhabited island in the Mediterranean sits a boat ride from the coast near Alicante, with a tiny walled old town, a handful of seafood restaurants and some of the clearest water on this stretch of coastline. I book this as a full day out for clients who want a change of pace partway through their stay.
