MADEIRA, PORTUGAL

Madeira gets overlooked by a lot of people booking a short haul trip, and every client I send there comes back surprised by how dramatic it actually is. Jagged green mountains, a coastline of cliffs and natural swimming pools rather than long sandy beaches, levada paths cut centuries ago to carry water across the island, and a mild climate that barely changes from January to December. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Madeira, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Madeira

Madeira is sometimes called the island of eternal spring, and once you have been there it makes complete sense. The volcanic coastline means proper sandy beaches are thin on the ground, so swimming here usually means a natural lava pool, a purpose built lido complex, or one of the handful of imported sand beaches at Calheta and Machico, not rows of sun loungers on golden sand. What you get instead is genuinely spectacular scenery, some of the best walking in Europe along the old levada water channels, Funchal's historic old town and markets, and a climate so mild and stable that it barely shifts between summer and winter.

I compare prices across the suppliers I work with for flights, hotels and transfers to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whether that is a famous clifftop icon, a modern seafront resort on the Lido promenade, or a quiet adults only escape.

Jake's top tip

I always ask a client why they fancy Madeira before I start looking at hotels. If it is walking, gardens and scenery they are after, almost anywhere works. If they are picturing a classic beach holiday with a long stretch of sand, I am honest that Madeira is not that, and I point them towards somewhere like Tenerife or the Algarve instead, or towards Calheta on Madeira's own south west coast, which has the island's best imported sand beach.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is the most commonly booked length, long enough to base yourself in or near Funchal, fit in a couple of levada walks or organised tours, and still have time to just relax by a pool. Because the flight is short for what you get, I also book plenty of four and five night breaks here, particularly for walkers and couples wanting a long weekend of proper scenery rather than a city break. At the other end, Madeira's genuinely mild winters mean a good number of my older clients book two, three or even four week stays between November and March purely for the climate, without ever touching a beach.

It suits walkers, garden and nature lovers, and couples wanting somewhere a bit different from the usual Spanish or Greek resort, down to the ground. It is also one of my go to suggestions for clients who want guaranteed mild weather over winter without a long haul flight. Families do visit, especially around the Lido lido complexes and purpose built sea water pools in Funchal, and Calheta's sand beach in particular is popular with parents, but I am always upfront that this is not a destination to book if a long sandy beach with sun loungers is the main thing on the wish list.

Getting there

Madeira Airport, officially Cristiano Ronaldo Madeira International Airport and still usually written as Funchal Airport, code FNC, is famous in its own right. The runway is partly built out over the sea on huge concrete stilts, since there was never enough flat land on the island to extend it any other way. Direct flights run from a good range of UK airports, among them London Gatwick, Luton and Stansted, Manchester, Edinburgh, Belfast, Liverpool and Bristol, with flight time of around three hours forty five minutes to four hours depending on where you fly from. The transfer from the airport into Funchal itself is short, usually twenty to twenty five minutes by road.

!
Jake's top tip

A good number of UK airports run flights to Madeira right through the winter as well as summer, not just as a seasonal sun route. That is exactly why I book so many client trips here for a mild winter break when the rest of mainland Europe has turned cold and grey.

Jake's top tip

Funchal sits in a natural suntrap on the south coast, while the north coast and the mountains in the middle of the island catch far more cloud and rain. Whichever hotel area I book for a client, I always point out that a short drive can mean a completely different sky.

Best time to visit

Because the climate barely swings across the year, Madeira does not really have an off season in the way a lot of Mediterranean resorts do. April through October is warmest and driest and the better window for walking and swimming in the natural pools. Spring brings the island's flower festival in Funchal, usually around April or May, with parades of flower covered floats through the city. New Year's Eve is another huge draw, with Funchal's fireworks display over the harbour once recognised by Guinness World Records as the largest in the world, and hotels book up well ahead for it. For a genuinely mild winter break with none of the long haul flight time, I point a lot of clients towards Madeira between November and March, when daytime temperatures are still comfortably in the mid teens.

Where to stay

Five real, bookable picks around Funchal, from a legendary clifftop icon to a modern adults only escape.

Funchal icon, clifftop gardens

Reid's Palace, A Belmond Hotel

Madeira's most famous hotel, open since 1891 on its own clifftop just outside Funchal's centre, with terraced subtropical gardens, several pools, a private sea bathing platform reached by its own lift and cable car, and the island's best known afternoon tea. A genuine occasion stay for clients wanting a slice of old world glamour. I can price this one up for you.

Best seafront, Lido area

Melia Madeira Mare Resort & Spa

A smart five star resort right on the Lido seafront, with sea water pools, sun terraces and a spa, a short walk from Funchal's Lido lido complex and a regular bus ride from the old town. A reliable choice for couples and families who want a genuine resort feel. I can price this one up for you.

Best adults only, Lido area

TUI Blue Gardens, Adults Only, Savoy Signature

A smaller, quieter adults only hotel set in subtropical gardens near the Lido, part of the Savoy group, with an outdoor pool and an easy stroll to Funchal's seafront promenade. The pick for couples who want calm over entertainment. I can price this one up for you.

Best boutique, Old Town

Castanheiro Boutique Hotel

A stylish boutique hotel tucked into Funchal's Old Town, with a rooftop pool and sun terrace above the city's rooftops, and the painted door lanes and the Mercado dos Lavradores farmers market right on the doorstep. My pick for couples who want to be in the thick of it on foot. I can price this one up for you.

Best modern adults only, Lido promenade

Allegro Madeira, Adults Only

A modern adults only hotel directly on the Lido promenade, part of the Barcelo group, with sea view pools and easy access to Funchal's sea water lido complex next door. A dependable, well located choice for couples who want a simple, modern base. I can price this one up for you.

Getting around

Funchal itself is walkable, with the Old Town, the seafront promenade and the Lido all reachable on foot from most central hotels, and a cable car links the centre up to Monte. Horarios do Funchal buses cover the city and reach out to villages across the island cheaply, and taxis are easy to find. A hire car is the easiest way to see the west coast, Porto Moniz and the mountain viewpoints at your own pace, though the roads are steep and winding, with a fair few tunnels. Many of the best levada walks start well outside Funchal, so I book organised minibus tours with transfers and guides included for clients who would rather not navigate remote trailheads and parking themselves.

Places to visit

Cabo Girao & Camara de Lobos

One of Europe's highest sea cliffs, with a glass floored skywalk jutting out nearly 600 metres above the ocean. Just along the coast sits Camara de Lobos, a small fishing village that reportedly once inspired Winston Churchill to take up painting on a visit, still full of brightly painted fishing boats.

Porto Moniz's natural pools

On the island's wild north west tip, volcanic rock pools filled by the Atlantic have been shaped into a series of natural swimming pools, with a modern complex of walkways and sun terraces built around them. One of the best examples of how Madeira does a swim without a sandy beach.

Santana's traditional houses

A hillside village on the north coast known for its palheiros, small triangular thatched houses with brightly painted doors that once housed farming families and now mostly survive as a local curiosity and photo stop on a round the island day out.

Pico Ruivo & the levada network

Madeira's highest peak at over 1,800 metres, usually reached on a half day walk above the clouds, and just one entry point into the island's famous levada network, old irrigation channels with paths alongside them that now rank among the best walking routes in Europe.
