IBIZA, SPAIN

Ibiza gets reduced to one word more than almost any destination I book, clubbing, but the island behind that reputation is a genuinely varied one. There is the famous side, San Antonio's sunset strip and the superclubs around Playa d'en Bossa, and there is the quieter side too, a UNESCO listed old town, pine backed coves on the north and east coast, and family resorts that have nothing to do with nightlife at all. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Ibiza, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Ibiza

Ibiza's reputation was built on its nightlife, and for a lot of clients that is still exactly why they are booking it, San Antonio and Playa d'en Bossa are home to some of the biggest clubs in the world and that scene is not going anywhere. What surprises most people I speak to is how different the rest of the island feels. Dalt Vila, Ibiza Town's walled old quarter, is one of the best preserved old towns in the Mediterranean, the north and east coasts around Portinatx and Santa Eulalia are genuinely calm and family friendly, and Cala Llonga and Es Vedra give the island a proper luxury, scenic side as well.

I compare prices across the suppliers I work with for flights, hotels and transfers to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whether that is a lively base near the clubs, a family resort on a quiet bay, or a longer multi centre trip taking in Formentera, Mallorca or Barcelona as well.

Jake's top tip

The single biggest question I ask every Ibiza client is which part of the island they actually want to be based in. San Antonio and Playa d'en Bossa and somewhere like Santa Eulalia or Portinatx are a world apart from each other, and getting that choice right matters more here than on almost any other island I book.

Trip length & who it suits

Four to seven nights is the classic Ibiza trip, long enough for a few club nights or a few lazy beach days, a trip to Dalt Vila, and a boat trip or Formentera day out, without feeling rushed. Clients combining Ibiza with Formentera, Mallorca or Barcelona, which I cover further down, tend to book nine nights or more.

It is genuinely one of the widest ranging islands I book. Groups of friends in their twenties head for San Antonio or Playa d'en Bossa for the clubs and beach clubs. Families do very well around Santa Eulalia, Portinatx or San Antonio Bay, where the pace is slower and a good number of hotels are built with kids in mind. Couples looking for something smarter tend to end up around Cala Llonga or the quieter coves near Ibiza Town. I would never describe Ibiza as a single type of holiday, it genuinely depends which part of the island you choose.

Getting there

Ibiza Airport, code IBZ, sits around seven kilometres from Ibiza Town and roughly fifteen to twenty minutes from San Antonio or Playa d'en Bossa, and thirty five to forty five minutes from Santa Eulalia or Portinatx on the other side of the island. Flight time from the UK runs at around two hours twenty to two hours forty, depending on which airport you fly from.

Like a number of the Mediterranean islands I book, direct UK routes to Ibiza are mostly seasonal rather than year round, generally running from around April through to October to match the island's own busiest months. A handful of airlines do add or drop routes from season to season, so I always check what is actually flying for your specific dates before we go any further.

!
Jake's top tip

If nightlife is the whole point of your trip, I always ask whether you want to be a short stagger from the clubs in San Antonio or Playa d'en Bossa, or whether you would rather have a quieter base and taxi or bus in for the big nights. Both work, it just changes which hotels I look at first.

Jake's top tip

October brings the heaviest rainfall of the year on Ibiza, so if you are chasing guaranteed sunshine I would steer you towards June through September instead and leave October for a quieter, cheaper trip where a shower or two is a fair trade off.

Best time to visit

June, July, August and September are Ibiza in full swing, the clubs, beach clubs and sunset bars are all open and the sea is at its warmest, though it is also when the island is busiest and most expensive. Late May and early June, plus late September into October, give you most of that same warmth and atmosphere with noticeably thinner crowds and lower prices, and this is genuinely my favourite window to book for clients who want sunshine without the peak season price tag. Winter, from November through to around April, is a different island altogether, a real number of hotels, clubs and restaurants close completely, so I would not suggest Ibiza as a winter sun destination, it is very much a spring through autumn trip.

Where to stay

Four real, bookable picks across the island, from a lively resort near the clubs to a quieter family base and a boutique coastal escape.

Best large resort, Playa d'en Bossa

Grand Palladium White Island Resort & Spa

A big, polished resort right on Playa d'en Bossa, Ibiza's longest beach and the stretch that holds several of the island's biggest clubs. Marble topped lobbies, palm lined gardens, a large tiled pool and a proper spa, with easy access to the superclubs when you want them and a genuine resort to retreat to when you do not. I can price this one up for you.

Best family base, San Antonio Bay

Alua Hawaii Ibiza

A four star hotel right on Es Puet beach in San Antonio Bay, built around a seaside pool with a dedicated mini club and kids' pool for families, plus a separate adult only terrace and chill out bar for the grown ups to escape to. A sensible, well located family pick on the same side of the island as San Antonio's nightlife, without being in the thick of it. I can price this one up for you.

Best quieter escape, Santa Eulalia

Iberostar Santa Eulalia

Set in its own pine grove overlooking s'Argamassa beach on the calmer east coast, with sea view rooms, a Mediterranean restaurant and a genuinely relaxed pace a world away from San Antonio. One of my go to picks for couples or families who want Ibiza's beaches and food without any of its nightlife reputation. I can price this one up for you.

Best boutique luxury, Cala Llonga

Mondrian Ibiza

A chic five star beach hotel on Cala Llonga, on the quieter coastline between Ibiza Town and Santa Eulalia, with a striking design led look, a beachfront pool and a genuinely sophisticated feel. The pick for clients who want Ibiza's design credentials and a proper beach setting, without basing themselves anywhere near the clubs. I can price this one up for you.

Getting around

A regular bus network connects Ibiza Town, San Antonio, Santa Eulalia and most of the resorts in between, and is a cheap, straightforward way to get around if you are not driving yourself. Taxis are easy enough to find in the main resorts, though they get busier and pricier on the biggest club nights. Hiring a car for a few days is popular and genuinely worth it if you want to explore the quieter north of the island, the beaches around Es Vedra, or the inland villages, since a lot of Ibiza's best spots are not close to any bus route. In Ibiza Town itself, the old port and Dalt Vila are easy enough to explore entirely on foot.

Places to visit

Dalt Vila

Ibiza Town's walled, UNESCO listed old quarter, built up the hillside above the harbour, with a cathedral at the top and genuinely beautiful views back over the port. A world away from the island's nightlife reputation, and one of the best preserved old towns anywhere on the Mediterranean.

Es Vedra

A dramatic, almost four hundred metre rocky islet rising out of the sea off Ibiza's southwest coast, wrapped in local legend and genuinely one of the most photographed views on the island, especially at sunset from the clifftop viewpoints or Cala d'Hort beach below.

A day trip to Formentera

A fast ferry of around twenty five to thirty minutes from Ibiza Town brings you to Formentera, a smaller, quieter island with some of the whitest sand and clearest water in the Mediterranean. I book this as a full day trip for a huge number of clients who want to see both islands.

San Antonio's Sunset Strip

A string of beach bars along San Antonio's west facing bay, Cafe del Mar and Cafe Mambo among the best known, built almost entirely around watching the sun go down with a drink in hand. Busy and a little pricey in peak season, but a proper Ibiza institution all the same.

Multi centre: combining Ibiza with somewhere else

Ibiza sits close to the other Balearic Islands and within easy flying distance of mainland Spain, which makes it a genuinely easy base to extend into a longer multi centre trip. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

Ibiza & Formentera

A short ferry of around twenty five to thirty minutes links the two islands, swapping Ibiza's energy for Formentera's much quieter pace and some of the best beaches in the Mediterranean. I would base you somewhere on Formentera's own coast for a few nights either side of your Ibiza stay, rather than treating it purely as a day trip.

Ibiza & Mallorca

A short flight of around thirty five minutes, or a longer ferry crossing, reaches Mallorca, the largest of the Balearic Islands, with Palma's old town, cathedral and far more varied countryside to explore. I would base you in Palma itself for the Mallorca leg, giving you a proper city and beach contrast to Ibiza.

Ibiza & Barcelona

A short flight of around an hour brings you to mainland Spain, where the Sagrada Familia, Park Guell and Barcelona's tapas bars make for a proper city break to pair with the island leg. I would base you centrally in Barcelona for easy access to the main sights on foot or by metro.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.
