SANTORINI, GREECE

Whitewashed houses and blue domed churches clinging to the rim of a volcanic caldera, with the sea dropping away hundreds of feet below and one of the best sunsets anywhere in Europe waiting at the end of the day. Santorini is the single most requested Greek island on my books, especially for honeymoons and anniversary trips, and it genuinely earns the hype. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Santorini, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Santorini

Santorini is a volcanic island, and that is really the whole story behind why it looks the way it does. A huge eruption thousands of years ago collapsed the centre of the island into the sea, leaving the dramatic cliff the main towns are built along today, with the caldera itself still an active, if quiet, volcano. Fira and Oia get the crowds and the photographs, but I always tell clients the island rewards slowing down, with quieter black and red sand beaches, proper working wineries and some of the best seafood in Greece once you get away from the clifftop restaurants.

I compare prices across the suppliers I work with for flights, hotels and transfers to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whether that is a week based entirely in Santorini or a longer multi centre trip taking in Mykonos, Athens or Crete as well.

Jake's top tip

Santorini is genuinely one of the smallest islands I book, so even clients who have never been tend to find their bearings within a day or two. The flip side is that it gets properly busy in peak summer, so I always talk clients through which towns and months give the calmer version of the same views.

Trip length & who it suits

Four to seven nights is the classic Santorini trip, long enough for Fira, Oia, a boat trip out to the volcano and a day or two by the pool or on the beach, without feeling like you are rushing to see everything. Clients combining Santorini with Mykonos, Athens or Crete, which I cover further down, tend to book nine nights or more so each place gets a proper look in.

It suits couples and honeymooners above almost anything else, which is exactly why so many of the island's hotels are built around caldera view suites, private plunge pools and sunset dining rather than big family resorts. That said, it is not purely a couples island. Families who base themselves in Kamari or Perissa, on the flatter, more laid back side of the island with proper sandy and black sand beaches, do very well too, they just will not find much in the way of kids' clubs or large all inclusive resorts here, since that is simply not the style Santorini builds for.

Getting there

Santorini (Thira) Airport, code JTR, sits inland near Kamari and Monolithos, around twenty to twenty five minutes from Fira and thirty five to forty five minutes from Oia. Direct flights run from a good number of UK airports, including London Gatwick, Manchester, Stansted, Birmingham and Edinburgh, with easyJet, Jet2, TUI and other carriers operating routes. Flight time is around three and a half to four hours.

Unlike some of the other Greek islands and the Spanish coast, Santorini is not really a year round flight destination. UK routes are seasonal, running from around April or May through to October, which matches the island's own season, since a genuine number of hotels, restaurants and tour operators close for winter and reopen around Easter. I always flag this to clients before they book anything outside the summer months.

!
Jake's top tip

If sunset views are the whole point of your trip, I always ask whether you want to be based in Oia itself, which gets genuinely packed with visitors every evening, or whether a short taxi or bus ride from Fira or Imerovigli for the same sunset with a fraction of the crowd suits you better.

Jake's top tip

The sea is warmest in August and September, but the island itself can be genuinely windy in July and August thanks to the meltemi winds that blow through the Aegean, so I always pack an extra layer for evenings even in the middle of summer.

Best time to visit

Late May, June and September are my favourite months to recommend, with warm, swimmable sea, reliably sunny weather and noticeably thinner crowds in Fira and Oia than peak summer. July and August are the hottest, busiest and most expensive months, with the biggest crowds at the sunset spots, so I always book those well ahead if clients want those specific dates. Unlike some of the other destinations I book, Santorini genuinely does wind down over winter. A real number of hotels, restaurants and the smaller tour boats close completely from around November through to March or early April, so this is not a destination I would suggest for a winter sun trip, it is very much an April through October island.

Getting around

A local bus network (KTEL) runs regularly from Fira's main station out to Oia, Kamari, Perissa and most of the other villages, and is the cheapest way to get around if you are not planning on driving yourself. Taxis exist but the island only has a limited number of them, so they can be hard to find and pricier at busy times, especially around sunset in Oia. Hiring a car, quad bike or ATV for a few days is popular and gives you the most flexibility to reach quieter beaches and wineries at your own pace. In Fira itself, a cable car runs down to the old port, with the alternative of walking, or riding a donkey, down the five hundred and eighty eight steps cut into the cliff.

Places to visit

Oia & the Sunset

The village everyone pictures when they think of Santorini, built along the very northern tip of the caldera, with its own castle ruins as the classic sunset viewpoint. It gets genuinely crowded by early evening in summer, so I always suggest arriving well ahead or watching from a quieter spot nearby instead.

Akrotiri

A remarkably well preserved Bronze Age settlement near the southern tip of the island, buried and protected by the same volcanic eruption that shaped the caldera, sometimes nicknamed the Minoan Pompeii. A genuinely interesting half day for anyone with an interest in history, a world away from the clifftop towns.

Red Beach

A striking, dramatic cove near Akrotiri, backed by steep red volcanic cliffs that give the beach its name. The walk down is a little uneven, and it gets busy in peak season, but it is one of the most photographed beaches on the island for good reason.

Santorini's Wineries

Santorini's volcanic soil produces genuinely distinctive wines, grown low to the ground in basket shaped vines to survive the island's winds. A handful of wineries around Megalochori and Pyrgos run tastings with caldera views thrown in, and I book this as a half day trip for a lot of couples.

Multi centre: combining Santorini with somewhere else

Santorini sits in the middle of the Cyclades, within easy ferry reach of the other Greek islands and a short flight from Athens, which makes it a genuinely easy base to extend into a longer multi centre trip. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

Santorini & Mykonos

A high speed ferry of around two to two and a half hours links the two most famous Cycladic islands, swapping Santorini's caldera views for Mykonos's whitewashed lanes, windmills and livelier beach clubs. I would base you at Mykonos Blu, A Grecotel Resort, on Psarou beach for a few nights either side of your Santorini stay.

Santorini & Athens

A short domestic flight of around fifty minutes, or a longer overnight ferry, brings you to Athens, where the Acropolis, the Plaka district and the National Archaeological Museum make for a proper city break to pair with the island leg. I would base you at Grecotel Pallas Athena, a smart boutique hotel right in the centre of the city.

Santorini & Crete

A ferry of around two to two and a half hours reaches Heraklion, Crete's largest city, within easy reach of the Palace of Knossos and a much bigger, greener island than Santorini. I would base you at Capsis Astoria Heraklion, right by the old Venetian harbour, for the Crete leg of the trip.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.
