KOS, GREECE

Kos is one of those Greek islands clients ask for by name without always knowing why, and the reason usually becomes obvious the moment they land. It is one of the flattest islands in Greece, so easy to cycle across, scattered with genuine ancient history going back to Hippocrates himself, ringed with long sandy beaches, and only a short ferry hop from two completely different multi centre options. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Kos, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Kos

Kos sits in the Dodecanese, the most easterly of Greece's main island groups, and it mixes its holiday crowd rather well across the island. Kardamena on the south coast is the lively, younger end of things, with its strip of bars and a long sandy beach. Tigaki and Marmari on the north coast are shallow, calm and sandy, which is exactly why I book so many families in there. Kos Town itself has the harbour, the castle, the famous plane tree and a proper old town feel without ever losing its holiday buzz, and the quieter Kefalos peninsula at the far south west end suits couples and windsurfers who want a bit of distance from it all. Hippocrates, generally regarded as the father of modern medicine, was born on Kos, and the ancient healing sanctuary he trained at is still standing and open to visit.

Jake's top tip

The first question I ask a Kos client is simply who is coming. Kardamena for a lively group of friends, Tigaki or Marmari for families with young kids and a shallow entry into the sea, Kos Town for couples who want history and restaurants on the doorstep, and Kefalos for anyone who wants the island's quietest corner.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is by far the most common length I book for Kos, long enough for a proper mix of beach time, a couple of tours and a night or two out, and I book plenty of ten and fourteen night stays for families making the most of the flight. Because Kos runs on seasonal flights roughly from spring through to autumn rather than all year round, it is not really a short break or winter sun destination in the way some of the Spanish islands are, so I steer clients towards a full week or longer rather than three or four nights.

It suits an unusually wide spread of travellers. Families love the shallow, sandy north coast beaches at Tigaki and Marmari. Groups of friends and younger couples gravitate towards Kardamena's nightlife. History minded couples get real value out of Kos Town, with the Asklepion, the castle and the old town all within easy reach. Because the island is so flat, I also book a fair number of keen cyclists and walkers here, and Kos works brilliantly as the start of a longer trip for anyone wanting to add Kalymnos, Bodrum or Patmos on as well.

Getting there

Kos International Airport, known locally as Hippokratis Airport, code KGS, sits roughly in the middle of the island. Direct flights run from a wide range of UK airports, seasonally from around April through to October, with flight time of around three hours forty five minutes to four hours fifteen minutes depending on where you fly from. Transfer times from the airport vary by resort, roughly ten minutes to Kos Town, fifteen to twenty minutes to Tigaki or Marmari, twenty to twenty five minutes to Kardamena, and up to thirty five to forty minutes down to Kefalos.

!
Jake's top tip

Because Kos is a seasonal flight destination rather than a year round one, the best priced seats and hotel rooms for the popular July and August weeks get booked up early. If a client already knows their dates, I always say to get it booked as soon as the season's flights go on sale rather than waiting.

July and August touch the low thirties most afternoons and Kardamena in particular is at its busiest and loudest, so for clients who want the heat without quite so much crowd, I point a lot of them towards June or September instead, when the sea is just as warm.

Best time to visit

May, June and September give the best balance of reliable heat, a warm sea and thinner crowds, and these are the months I steer most of my flexible clients towards. July and August are hottest and busiest, especially around Kardamena's nightlife strip, and are the obvious choice for families tied to the school holidays. April and October are noticeably quieter and cooler, but still pleasant enough for walking, cycling and sightseeing around the Asklepion and Kos Town, with the sea still carrying some warmth from summer into October thanks to its thermal lag. Because Kos runs on seasonal flights, there is no real winter season here at all, so clients looking for a winter sun trip need to look elsewhere.

Getting around

Kos is one of the flattest islands in Greece, which makes it genuinely popular for cycling, and bike hire is cheap and easy to find in every resort. Local buses connect Kos Town with the main resorts along both coasts, and taxis are easy to find, though worth booking ahead in peak season. A hire car is the easiest way to reach the Kefalos peninsula, Zia village up on Mount Dikeos, and the Asklepion at your own pace. Kos Town's own port is also the departure point for day trips and ferries across to Kalymnos, Nisyros, Pserimos and Bodrum in Turkey.

Multi centre: combining Kos with somewhere else

Kos sits right at the edge of the Dodecanese chain and within easy ferry reach of Turkey's Aegean coast, which makes it a genuinely easy base to extend into a longer multi centre trip. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

Kos & Kalymnos

A short local ferry of around thirty to forty five minutes reaches Kalymnos, known across Europe as a rock climbing destination and for its sponge diving history, and a much quieter, more traditional island than Kos. I would base you at MasouriBlu Boutique Hotel on the west coast at Masouri, close to the climbing crags and the beach, for the Kalymnos leg of the trip.

Kos & Bodrum, Turkey

A fast catamaran ferry of around forty five minutes to an hour from Kos Town's port crosses into Turkey at Bodrum, swapping whitewashed Greek island lanes for Bodrum Castle, a buzzing marina and the bazaar. I would base you at Doria Hotel Bodrum, in the quieter bay of Bitez just outside the town, for the Bodrum leg of the trip.

Kos & Patmos

A longer ferry of around two and a half to three hours, often calling at Kalymnos or Leros on the way, reaches Patmos, the island where Saint John the Theologian is said to have written the Book of Revelation, home to the UNESCO listed Monastery of Saint John and the Cave of the Apocalypse. A total change of pace from Kos's resorts. I would base you at Patmos Aktis, A Luxury Collection Resort & Spa, near Skala, for the Patmos leg of the trip.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.

Places to visit

The Asklepion

An ancient healing sanctuary set across terraced hillside just outside Kos Town, linked to Hippocrates himself and regarded as one of the birthplaces of modern medicine. The ruins of the temples and treatment halls are still standing, with views back down over Kos Town and across to the Turkish coast.

Zia Village & Mount Dikeos

A traditional mountain village known across the island for its sunset views, with tavernas, honey and herb shops lining the main lane. It also sits at the foot of Mount Dikeos, Kos's highest point, where a path leads up through forest to the summit for anyone wanting a proper walk.

Kos Town's Castle & the Hippocrates Plane Tree

The Castle of the Knights guards the harbour entrance, built by the Knights of St John, while in the square just below it stands the vast, centuries old Hippocrates Plane Tree, long claimed locally as the spot where Hippocrates once taught his students, now one of the oldest trees in Europe.

Tigaki & the north coast beaches

A long, shallow, sandy beach on the north coast, backed by a salt lake that draws flamingos at certain times of year, and one of the most reliable spots on the island for families with young children thanks to how gently the sea shelves.
