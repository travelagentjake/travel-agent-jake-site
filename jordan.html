JORDAN

Petra carved into rose coloured rock, the dunes of Wadi Rum and floating in the Dead Sea, all in one trip. Here's exactly how I book Jordan, the real tours that cover it and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Jordan

Jordan is one of those destinations that sells itself the moment a client sees a photo of the Treasury at Petra, and it genuinely lives up to it in person. Jordan isn't really sold as a flight and hotel package. It's booked a different way, as a real escorted tour that strings Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea together, flights, hotels, transport and a lot of the sightseeing already built in.

That suits Jordan well, since it isn't really a destination you'd want to self plan on a first visit. The distances between the big sights are longer than people expect, public transport is limited outside the cities, and a lot of the best bits, the 4x4 run through Wadi Rum, the walk into Petra through the Siq, only really work with a guide who knows the route and the history.

!
Jake's top tip

I always set expectations early that Jordan is a touring holiday, not a beach week with a day trip bolted on. Clients who go in expecting that get the best out of it.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven to eight days, six or seven nights, is the standard length for a proper Jordan tour, enough time to cover Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea without rushing between them. It suits couples, friends and small groups who want a genuine cultural and adventure trip rather than pure relaxation, and who are happy with early starts, a fair amount of walking and some uneven ground, especially at Petra and in the desert at Wadi Rum. It's less suited to anyone with limited mobility or very young children, and it isn't really a lie on a sunbed holiday, though most tours do build in some proper down time floating in the Dead Sea.

Getting there

British Airways and Royal Jordanian both fly direct from London Heathrow to Amman, a flight of around 5 hours 20 minutes, and return flights are included in the price of every tour I book. UK passport holders need a visa for Jordan, either a visa on arrival for 40 Jordanian dinars, about £42, valid for 3 months, or the Jordan Pass, 70 to 80 dinars depending on the sites it covers, about £75 to £85, which includes the visa fee and discounted entry to Petra and other paid sites. The Jordan Pass has to be bought online before you travel and only pays off if you're staying at least 3 nights, which every tour I sell comfortably covers. Your passport needs at least 6 months left on it from your arrival date.

!
Jake's top tip

I always tell clients to buy the Jordan Pass through the official jordanpass.jo website only, and to sort it before they fly since it needs to be arranged in advance, not at the airport. It almost always works out cheaper than paying for Petra entry and the visa separately.

Jordan's climate changes a lot depending on elevation. Amman and Petra sit high up and feel genuinely cold in winter, while the Dead Sea, Wadi Rum and Aqaba sit much lower and run noticeably hotter all year round. These are long term averages for Amman, the usual starting point for every tour.

Best time to visit

March to May and September to November are my pick for most clients, warm days, cooler evenings and none of the extreme heat. June to August is properly hot everywhere, and genuinely intense by midday at Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea, though tours build in early starts for exactly that reason and it's still a popular time to go. Winter, December to February, is a different trip at altitude, cold and sometimes wet in Amman and Petra with the odd flurry of snow, but the Dead Sea and Aqaba sit so much lower that they stay noticeably milder, so a tour that ends there still has a warm few days to finish on even in the depths of a UK winter.

!
Jake's top tip

If a client is set on floating in the Dead Sea in genuinely warm weather, I steer them towards spring or autumn. Winter still works for the Dead Sea itself, it's Amman and Petra at the start of the trip that feel the cold.

Three ways to see Jordan

Jordan is a natural multi centre trip by design, every real tour strings Amman, Petra, Wadi Rum and the Dead Sea together in one booking. Here are three real, bookable tours so you can see which pace and style actually fits.

The same 8 day Classical Jordan tour, extended with a 4 day, 3 night stay by the Red Sea in Aqaba. The one to pick if you'd rather finish on a beach than fly straight home from the Dead Sea. From £2,342pp.

Where to stay

Because these are escorted tours rather than pick your own hotel holidays, the hotels come as part of the route, the same as other escorted tours such as Japan or China. The Jordan and Petra tour uses the Centro Mada Amman, a Bedouin style tented camp at Wadi Rum, the 4 star Old Village Hotel & Resort in Wadi Musa for Petra, and the 4 star Holiday Inn Dead Sea in Sweimah. The Classical Jordan tour, and its Aqaba extension, uses the Mena Tyche Hotel in Amman, the Hayat Zaman Hotel at Petra, the en suite tented Magic Rum Camp at Wadi Rum, and the Crowne Plaza Dead Sea Resort & Spa. Hotels on both tours are provisional until closer to departure, so I always confirm the exact named properties once a specific date is booked.

!
Jake's top tip

The night in the desert camp is a genuine highlight for most clients, not a downgrade. En suite tented camps like Magic Rum Camp have proper beds and bathrooms, it's just a very different, and very memorable, kind of night under the stars.

Getting around

Every transfer, 4x4 run through Wadi Rum and coach journey between cities is arranged and included once you're on tour, with an English speaking guide or driver guide throughout, so there's nothing to organise yourself day to day. Independent travel between Jordan's main sights is genuinely harder than in most of the destinations I sell, public transport is limited outside Amman, distances are longer than they look on a map, and taxis between cities aren't cheap, the JETT bus company runs a limited intercity service between Amman, Petra and Aqaba for independent travellers, but it's nowhere near as convenient as having everything arranged for you. It's one of the clearest cases I come across of a tour genuinely being the sensible way to see a country, rather than just the easiest.

Places to visit

Petra

A UNESCO listed city carved into rose coloured sandstone, reached on foot through the narrow Siq gorge before the Treasury suddenly opens up in front of you. The Monastery, a further climb up over 800 steps, is quieter and just as impressive.

Wadi Rum

A vast red desert of sandstone mountains and dunes, explored by 4x4 with a Bedouin guide and usually finished with a traditional zarb barbecue and a night under some of the darkest skies you'll see anywhere.

The Dead Sea

The lowest point on earth, over 9 times saltier than the ocean, so you float without trying. Most hotels have mineral rich mud free for guests to use too, a genuine highlight after a few days on your feet.

Jerash

One of the best preserved Roman provincial cities anywhere in the world, with a colonnaded main street, a huge oval forum and two amphitheatres, included on most Jordan tours as a day trip from Amman.

!
Jake's top tip

Jordan is a predominantly Muslim country, so alcohol is only really available in Western style hotel bars and restaurants in Amman and the main tourist areas, not everywhere. It's worth knowing before you go rather than being surprised on the ground.
