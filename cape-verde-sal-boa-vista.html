CAPE VERDE: SAL & BOA VISTA

Cape Verde is the winter sun destination I point people towards when they want proper warmth in December, January or February without a long haul flight. Around six hours from the UK, only an hour or two behind UK time, and built around wide sandy beaches and all inclusive resorts on two islands, Sal and Boa Vista. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Cape Verde, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Cape Verde

Cape Verde is a group of volcanic islands in the Atlantic, off the coast of West Africa, and almost every UK holiday here goes to one of two islands. Sal is the busier of the two, with the lively little town of Santa Maria sitting right on a long, pale beach and plenty of bars and restaurants within walking distance of the hotels. Boa Vista is quieter and wilder, with huge empty beaches, sand dunes and resorts that tend to feel more self contained. Both are flat, dry and sunny, and the landscape is more desert island than tropical, so do not expect palm fringed jungle.

I compare prices across the flights, hotels and transfers I have access to across the suppliers I work with, to find the best fit for your dates and budget, whether that is an adults only escape on Sal or a big family resort with a waterpark on Boa Vista.

!
Jake's top tip

The one thing I warn every client about is the wind. Cape Verde is breezy for much of the year, especially from roughly November to May, which is exactly why Sal is so popular for kitesurfing. It keeps the heat comfortable, but it can kick up sand on the beach, so a sheltered pool area and a decent spot on the beach make more difference here than almost anywhere else I book.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is the most common length for Cape Verde, and with a flight of around six hours it works well for a week of guaranteed warmth in the middle of a UK winter. Ten and fourteen night stays are popular too, especially over Christmas, New Year and February half term, when a lot of my clients want a proper break from the cold.

It suits couples and families who want an easy all inclusive beach holiday with reliable sunshine, and it is one of the best value winter sun options that does not involve a long haul flight. It also suits anyone into watersports, as Sal has a strong reputation for kitesurfing and windsurfing. It is less suited to anyone who wants lush scenery, lots of sightseeing or a big nightlife scene, as the islands are mostly about the beach, the resort and a handful of excursions.

Sal or Boa Vista? Sal suits clients who like to walk into a town in the evening, with Santa Maria's bars, restaurants and pier all close to the main hotel strip. Boa Vista suits clients who want to switch off completely, with longer and emptier beaches, sand dunes and resorts that are mostly further from town. Flights from the UK go direct to both, so the choice really comes down to the feel of holiday you are after.

Getting there

Direct flights from the UK run to both main holiday islands. Sal's Amilcar Cabral International Airport, code SID, is roughly fifteen to twenty minutes by road from the hotels around Santa Maria. Boa Vista's Aristides Pereira International Airport, code BVC, is close to the island's main town of Sal Rei, with most resort transfers taking around ten to thirty minutes. TUI Airways and easyJet fly direct from a range of UK airports, with flight times of around five and a half to six and a quarter hours depending on where you fly from and which island you are heading to.

!
Jake's top tip

Everyone flying into Cape Verde needs to register online through the government's EASE system before travelling and pay the airport security fee, currently 3,400 Cape Verdean Escudos per person, roughly £27, with children under two exempt. It can be paid online ahead of time or on arrival, but doing it before you fly saves queuing at the airport, so I always remind clients to sort it a few days before departure.

!
Jake's top tip

Twenty five degrees in January sounds modest on paper, but with strong sunshine and a breeze that hides how hot it really is, it is very easy to burn here. I always tell clients to pack high factor suncream and a light layer for the evenings, which can feel cool in the winter months once the sun goes down.

Best time to visit

November to May is the classic Cape Verde season for UK holidaymakers, dry, sunny and comfortably warm, and it is when the islands really come into their own as winter sun. Christmas, New Year and February half term are the busiest and priciest weeks, so they are worth booking early. This is also the windiest stretch of the year, which is perfect for kitesurfers and keeps the heat pleasant, though it can make some beach days breezy. June and July bring calmer winds and warmer temperatures, while August to October is the hottest and most humid time, with the occasional short downpour and the warmest sea. July to October is also loggerhead turtle nesting season, when guided turtle watching trips run on both Sal and Boa Vista.

An adults only, all inclusive resort right on the beach at Ponta Preta, just outside Santa Maria. Expect pools with a swim up bar, buffet and a la carte dining including an Asian bistro, and a full spa with a sauna, steam room and hot tub. My pick for couples wanting a calm, grown up week in the sun. I can price this one up for you.

Best for families, Sal

Hotel Riu Palace Santa Maria

A large, 24 hour all inclusive resort on the beach near Santa Maria, with several outdoor pools including one with a swim up bar, a children's pool with slides, the Splash Water World waterpark and the RiuLand kids club. Themed restaurants, a spa and a gym round it off. A genuine all rounder for families who want everything on site. I can price this one up for you.

Best for walking into town, Sal

Hilton Cabo Verde Sal Resort

A stylish beachfront hotel on Santa Maria Beach, a short stroll from the town's bars and restaurants. Two outdoor pools plus a children's pool, a handful of restaurants and bars including a beach club, and the Eforea spa. My pick for clients who want a polished hotel without being locked into an all inclusive bubble. I can price this one up for you.

Best premium all inclusive, Boa Vista

Hotel Riu Palace Boavista

A 24 hour all inclusive resort set beside a long sandy beach, with three outdoor pools and a children's pool, a main restaurant plus themed restaurants including a steakhouse, the Renova spa, and access to the Splash Water World waterpark. Every room has a balcony or terrace. My pick for a more upmarket stay on Boa Vista. I can price this one up for you.

Best value beachfront, Boa Vista

A big all inclusive resort directly on the beachfront at Santa Monica in the south of the island, one of Boa Vista's most beautiful and empty beaches. It also has adults only rooms with their own adults only pool, which makes it a handy fit for groups mixing families and couples. It is a long way from Sal Rei, so it suits clients happy to stay put. Getting around

On Sal, most hotels sit within walking distance of Santa Maria or a short, cheap taxi ride away, so many of my clients never need any transport beyond their transfer. Taxis are easy to find and usually work on agreed fares rather than meters, so settle the price before you set off. On Boa Vista the resorts are more spread out and many are a fair distance from Sal Rei, so a taxi into town for an evening is worth planning ahead. For seeing either island, the roads beyond the main towns are often rough tracks, which is why I always point clients towards an organised 4x4, buggy or quad tour rather than a hire car. Sal and Boa Vista are linked by short domestic flights, but schedules can change, so I would not plan a two island holiday without checking the current timetable first.

Places to visit

Pedra de Lume salt lake, Sal

Old salt pans set inside the crater of an extinct volcano on Sal's east coast, where the water is so salty you float effortlessly, a bit like the Dead Sea. It is the stop almost every island tour includes, and it is usually paired with the Buracona natural pool and its Blue Eye, a cave where sunlight lights up the water a vivid blue around midday.

Shark Bay & Santa Maria, Sal

At Shark Bay, a short drive north of Santa Maria, you wade into shallow water to watch young lemon sharks swimming around your feet, with water shoes recommended. Back in Santa Maria itself, the old pier is where local fishermen land their catch, and the little town has a relaxed mix of cafes, bars and craft stalls that is easy to wander in an evening.

Viana Desert & the Cabo Santa Maria wreck, Boa Vista

The Viana Desert is a sweep of pale sand dunes inland from Sal Rei, and the classic Boa Vista 4x4 or buggy tour combines it with the rusting wreck of the Cabo Santa Maria, a cargo ship that ran aground off Atalanta Beach in 1968. Many tours also stop in the village of Rabil, known for its pottery and handicrafts.

Turtle nesting season, both islands

From roughly July to October, loggerhead turtles come ashore on the beaches of Sal and Boa Vista to lay their eggs, and local conservation groups run small, guided night walks to watch them without disturbing the nests. It is one of the most memorable things you can do on a late summer or autumn trip, and numbers on each walk are limited, so it is worth booking early.

Cost of living: what things actually cost

Cape Verde's currency is the Cape Verdean Escudo (CVE), which is pegged to the euro, and euros are widely accepted in the tourist areas of Sal and Boa Vista. Most stays here are all inclusive, so day to day spending tends to be limited to excursions, drinks in town and souvenirs. !
Jake's top tip

Euros go a long way here, as most bars, taxis and excursion sellers in the tourist areas will take them, though change is often given in escudos. Bring some smaller euro notes for tips and taxis, and remember the airport security fee if you have not already paid it online.
