THAILAND

Phuket's beaches, Bangkok's temples, real multi centre options with Koh Samui and Khao Lak, weather by month and what it actually costs, everything you need to plan a trip to Thailand.

What Jake says about Thailand

Thailand is one of the biggest, most varied long haul trips I sell, and that variety is exactly why clients love it. Phuket gives you a proper beach resort holiday with genuinely excellent hotels, Bangkok gives you temples, markets and a skyline unlike anywhere else in Asia, and Koh Samui gives you a quieter, more laid back island feel. A lot of clients end up combining two of these rather than picking just one, and I'd encourage it if the budget and time allow.

It's currently a TUI only destination for me rather than Jet2holidays or easyJet holidays. Neither of those two flies long haul at the moment, so TUI is the operator I use for flights and hotel packages to Thailand, covering Phuket, Khao Lak, Koh Samui and Bangkok.

!
Jake's top tip

First time asking about Thailand? I always ask whether the client wants one base for the whole trip or would rather split it across two places. Phuket alone suits a relaxed, resort based holiday. Adding Bangkok or Koh Samui turns it into a proper two centre adventure.

Trip length & who it suits

Ten nights is the shortest length I'd really recommend given the flight time, and it's enough for a single base in Phuket with a few days to properly recover from the journey either side. Twelve to fourteen nights is the sweet spot for most clients, especially anyone combining Phuket with Bangkok or Koh Samui. Three weeks or more suits clients wanting a genuinely slow trip covering several areas, or combining Thailand with a wider South East Asia itinerary.

Thailand suits honeymooners, couples, families with children of any age thanks to resorts built with kids in mind, and independent travellers who want some structure around a hotel but also want to go off and explore temples, markets and islands under their own steam. It's less suited to clients wanting a short week away, since the flight time alone takes a decent chunk out of a week long trip, or to clients who want total predictability from their weather, given the monsoon swings between the Andaman and Gulf coasts.

Getting there

Flights to Phuket and Bangkok from the UK typically take around 12 to 13 hours, usually with one stop, though TUI runs direct charter flights to Phuket from several UK airports during the winter season, which is the option I book most often. Worth knowing for context: Virgin Atlantic launched a new non stop route from London Heathrow to Phuket on 18 October 2026, flying three times a week, the first UK direct scheduled service to the island. I don't currently book Thailand through Virgin Atlantic Holidays, but it's a sign of how much demand for Phuket has grown, and it's worth me keeping an eye on as an alternative option.

!
Jake's top tip

I always check whether a client's dates fall inside a TUI direct charter window to Phuket, since a direct flight makes a real difference over a 12 hour journey compared with a connection through the Gulf or further into Asia.

!
Jake's top tip

Koh Samui, on the Gulf coast, runs the opposite pattern to Phuket. Its driest months are roughly December to August, and its wettest spell is October to December, with November usually the wettest month of all. If a client is set on Koh Samui, I steer them away from November regardless of how Phuket's forecast looks.

Best time to visit

November to March is Thailand's prime season on the Andaman coast, with warm, reliably dry days, which makes it the busiest and most expensive time to book Phuket, Khao Lak or a Bangkok and Phuket combination. April is hot and still largely dry, and a good shoulder option, though it does coincide with the Songkran water festival, which some clients love and others prefer to avoid. May to October is the wet season, with September the wettest month, but showers are often short and heavy rather than all day, and hotel prices drop noticeably. For Koh Samui specifically, the best months flip to roughly December through August, with November best avoided given it's usually the wettest month on the Gulf coast.

!
Jake's top tip

Clients chasing value often ask about August. I'm upfront that rain is likely most days, usually in a short, heavy burst rather than ruining the whole day, and it's one of the cheapest, quietest months to go.

Where to stay

Phuket is the island most clients base themselves on, and which part of it you pick changes the whole feel of the holiday.

Patong & Kalim

The island's biggest, busiest resort town, with Bangla Road's nightlife, the widest choice of hotels and restaurants, and a long sandy beach right in the middle of it all. The pick for clients who want everything on their doorstep and don't mind a livelier atmosphere.

Kata & Karon

Two calmer, more family friendly beaches a short drive south of Patong, with a gentler pace, good swimming and a smaller but still solid choice of restaurants and bars. A strong middle ground for families and couples who still want some nightlife within reach.

Bang Tao, Laguna & Surin

Phuket's upmarket northwest coast, home to the Laguna Phuket integrated resort complex, Boat Avenue's restaurants and a run of five star hotels along a long, quieter beach. The pick for clients wanting a polished, resort heavy holiday with golf and spa facilities close by.

Rawai & Kata Noi

The island's quieter southern tip, with a slower, more local feel, excellent seafood and easy boat trips out to the Phi Phi Islands and Racha Islands. Suits clients who've been to Phuket before and want somewhere calmer, or anyone prioritising a quieter beach over nightlife.

!
Jake's top tip

Not sure which area to recommend? Patong for clients who want nightlife and choice right outside the hotel, Kata or Karon for families, and Bang Tao or Rawai for anyone wanting a quieter, more upmarket stay.

Getting around

Grab, the regional ride hailing app, is by far the easiest way to get around Phuket and Bangkok, with fixed upfront pricing and no haggling. Metered taxis exist but are less common outside Bangkok, and tuk-tuks are widely available, charge more than Grab for the same trip, and are worth agreeing a price with upfront. Songthaews, shared pickup truck taxis, run fixed routes between Phuket's beaches and Phuket Town for a flat low fare and are a cheap way to get around if a client doesn't mind a shared ride. Most clients on a package holiday won't need to hire a car at all, since airport transfers are included and Grab or taxis comfortably cover the rest, though self drive scooters are popular with confident riders despite Thailand's higher accident rates for tourists on two wheels.

Multi centre: combining Phuket with the rest of Thailand

Thailand is a natural multi centre destination, and a lot of my clients end up splitting their trip across two places rather than staying put in one. Here are three ways I'd put it together.

Bangkok & Phuket

Around 12 to 14 nights: 3 to 4 nights in Bangkok for the temples, the Grand Palace and the markets, followed by a week or more unwinding on Phuket's beaches. This is one of the most popular combinations I book, with a short internal flight of around 1 hour 20 minutes linking the two.

Bangkok & Koh Samui

Around 12 to 14 nights: a city stay in Bangkok followed by a week on Koh Samui's quieter, more laid back beaches, home to TUI BLUE The Passage Samui. A good alternative to Phuket for clients wanting a smaller, less built up island, bearing in mind Samui's own wetter months run October to December.

Phuket & Khao Lak

Around 10 to 14 nights, staying entirely on the Andaman coast: several nights in Phuket followed by a few days further north in the quieter, more low rise resort area of Khao Lak, home to TUI BLUE Khao Lak Resort. No flight needed, just a scenic drive of around 1 hour 30 minutes between the two.

These are built as separate hotel bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message Jake and he'll put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.

Places to visit

The Big Buddha

A 45 metre marble statue on top of Nakkerd Hill, with sweeping views across the south of the island and out to sea. Free to visit, though a short, fairly steep walk up from the car park, and best timed for a cooler part of the day.

Phuket Old Town

A grid of Sino Portuguese shophouses, street art and good cafes in Phuket Town, a completely different pace to the beach resorts and well worth a half day trip, especially on a Sunday when Lard Yai walking street market takes over the old quarter.

James Bond Island & Phang Nga Bay

A boat trip through Phang Nga Bay's dramatic limestone karsts, taking in the famous rock from The Man with the Golden Gun and usually a stop to paddle a canoe through sea caves. One of the most popular day trips booked out of Phuket.

An ethical elephant sanctuary

A half or full day visit to a no riding, feeding and bathing focused elephant sanctuary is one of the most memorable things clients do in Phuket. I always point clients toward ethical, no riding sanctuaries rather than older style elephant camps.
