DUBAI

Weather by month, where to stay, things to do, real multi centre options with Abu Dhabi and Ras Al Khaimah, and what it actually costs, everything you need to plan a trip to Dubai.

What Jake says about Dubai

Dubai is one of the easiest holidays I sell, because it genuinely suits almost everyone. Couples get the beach resorts and the skyline views, families get waterparks and indoor attractions that work even in the heat, and anyone who wants a short, reliable flight with guaranteed sunshine gets exactly that. It's also one of the few long haul destinations where a week doesn't feel rushed, since the flight itself is only around seven hours.

I compare Dubai packages for every client rather than sticking to one option, so you get the best package and price for your dates. There is an enormous range of hotels to choose from across every part of the city and every budget.

!
Jake's top tip

First time asking about Dubai? I always ask whether the client wants a beach resort with a pool and private stretch of sand, or a city hotel closer to the Burj Khalifa and Dubai Mall. The two feel like genuinely different holidays, so it's worth deciding early.

Trip length & who it suits

Four or five nights works well as a long weekend city break, especially if a client just wants the Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall and a bit of sun. Seven nights is the classic package length and gives enough time to properly relax at a beach resort as well as fit in a desert safari and a day in the city. Ten to fourteen nights suits anyone combining Dubai with Abu Dhabi or Ras Al Khaimah as a proper multi centre trip.

Dubai suits couples, honeymooners, families with children of any age, and anyone wanting a reliable short haul feeling holiday without the long haul flight time, since it's roughly the same flying time as the Caribbean but without crossing anywhere near as many time zones. It's less suited to clients on a tight budget who want to self cater and explore cheaply, since Dubai runs noticeably more expensive than Spain, Turkey or the Canaries once you add in food, attractions and taxis.

Getting there

Direct flights to Dubai International Airport run from London Heathrow, London Gatwick, Manchester, Birmingham, Newcastle and Glasgow, mostly with Emirates, with British Airways also flying from Heathrow. Flight time is around seven hours. A package includes return flights, transfers and your hotel in one price, so there's nothing extra to arrange once you land.

!
Jake's top tip

I always check whether a client prefers Dubai International Airport, which sits close to Downtown and Deira, or whether their hotel is out towards the Marina or Palm Jumeirah, since transfer times vary a fair bit depending on where in the city you're staying.

Best time to visit

November to March is Dubai's prime season, with warm, reliably dry days and comfortable evenings, which also makes it the busiest and most expensive time to book, especially over Christmas, New Year and the Dubai Shopping Festival. April and October either side of summer are genuinely good value shoulder months, still hot by UK standards but without peak season pricing. June to September is extremely hot and humid, with daytime highs regularly above 38 degrees, so it's the cheapest time to go but suits clients happy to spend their days by the pool, in the mall's air conditioning or on evening activities rather than out sightseeing all day.

!
Jake's top tip

Clients chasing the lowest possible price often ask about July and August. I'm always upfront that the heat is genuinely intense then, and a beach resort with a great pool matters more than ever if they book that window.

Where to stay

Dubai is spread out, and which part of the city you base yourself in changes the whole feel of the holiday.

Downtown Dubai & Business Bay

Home to the Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall and the Dubai Fountain, this is the best base for anyone who wants the skyline views and the city's biggest sights right outside the hotel door. It's less beach focused, so most hotels here have a rooftop or courtyard pool rather than direct sand.

Dubai Marina & JBR

A buzzy, walkable waterfront with the Marina Walk, countless restaurants and a proper public beach at Jumeirah Beach Residence. A good middle ground for clients who want both beach time and an evening out without needing a taxi for everything.

Palm Jumeirah

The famous palm shaped island, home to Dubai's biggest resort hotels, private beaches and the Burj Al Arab's own stretch of coastline nearby. The pick for clients wanting a proper resort holiday with a genuine beach, often at a premium price to match.

Deira & Bur Dubai

Old Dubai, around the Creek, the Gold Souk and the Spice Souk, with a more traditional, far less glossy feel than the rest of the city. Hotels here tend to be better value, and it suits clients more interested in culture and history than beach time.

!
Jake's top tip

Not sure which area to recommend? Palm Jumeirah or the Marina for a beach first holiday, Downtown for a city first trip, and Deira or Bur Dubai for anyone on a tighter budget who still wants to be close to the sights.

Getting around

The Dubai Metro is driverless, air conditioned, cheap and covers most of the places tourists actually want to reach, including a stop near Dubai Mall and a direct line to the airport. A rechargeable Nol card covers the metro, buses and the tram. Taxis are plentiful, metered and reasonably priced, and Careem and Uber both work well if a client would rather book a ride from an app. Traditional abras still cross Dubai Creek for a few dirhams and are a lovely, cheap way to see Old Dubai from the water. Most clients on a package holiday won't need to hire a car at all, since transfers between the airport and hotel are included, and taxis or the metro comfortably cover everything else.

Multi centre: combining Dubai with the rest of the UAE

Dubai is a natural multi centre destination, with two proper neighbouring emirates within easy reach and even its own beach and city sides worth splitting across two hotels. Here are three ways I'd put it together.

Dubai & Abu Dhabi

Around 7 to 10 nights: split between a Dubai base and 2 to 3 nights in Abu Dhabi, home to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, Louvre Abu Dhabi and Yas Island's theme parks. Abu Dhabi hotels such as Beach Rotana or Yas Island Rotana can be booked alongside your Dubai hotel. The drive between the two cities takes around 90 minutes.

Dubai & Ras Al Khaimah

Around 7 to 10 nights: a few nights in Dubai followed by 3 to 4 nights in the quieter, more dramatic landscape of Ras Al Khaimah, home to Jebel Jais and its world record breaking zipline. RAK hotels such as Rixos Bab Al Bahr or DoubleTree by Hilton Resort and Spa Marjan Island can be booked as part of the trip. The drive between the two is around 45 to 60 minutes.

Dubai split stay: city & beach

Around 7 to 10 nights, staying entirely within Dubai itself: a few nights in Downtown or Deira for the Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall and Old Dubai, then moving to the Marina or Palm Jumeirah for the second half for proper beach and resort time. No long transfer needed between the two, just a short taxi or metro ride.

These are built as separate hotel bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message Jake and he'll put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.

Places to visit

Burj Khalifa & The Dubai Fountain

The world's tallest building, with an observation deck on levels 124 and 125 and a pricier option up to level 148. Time a visit for sunset and stay on for the Dubai Fountain show outside, which runs every half hour in the evening and is free to watch from the promenade.

The Gold Souk & Dubai Creek

Old Dubai's maze of gold and spice traders, best reached by taking a cheap abra ride across the Creek rather than a taxi. A completely different pace and feel to the glass towers of Downtown, and one of the best free things to do in the whole city.

Palm Jumeirah & The Burj Al Arab

Even without staying on the Palm itself, the view of the famous sail shaped Burj Al Arab from Jumeirah Beach or Kite Beach is a Dubai essential, and afternoon tea inside is a proper treat for clients wanting to see it up close.

The desert & Jebel Jais

A desert safari with dune bashing, a camel ride and a barbecue dinner under the stars is close to a Dubai rite of passage. Clients adding Ras Al Khaimah can carry on to Jebel Jais, home to the world's longest zipline, for an even bigger adrenaline hit.
