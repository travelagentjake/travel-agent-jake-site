BLACK FRIDAY HOLIDAY DEALS: WHAT TO CHECK BEFORE YOU BOOK, AND THE SCAMS TO WATCH FOR

Black Friday and Cyber Monday are two of the busiest weeks of the year for holiday bookings, and fraudsters know it just as well as the genuine operators do. Fake comparison sites, copied hotel listings and too good to be true prices all spike around the big sale events. The reporting system behind it all has also just changed. Here's exactly how the scams actually work, the genuine checks that catch them out, and where to report one now that Action Fraud has become Report Fraud.

Three things worth knowing before this year's sales

None of this should put anyone off a genuine Black Friday holiday deal, there are usually some good ones about. It just pays to know what you're actually looking at.

The price trap

Unrealistic prices are the classic lure

Cloned comparison sites and fake listings almost always undercut the genuine price by a significant margin to get you to click and pay quickly.

The copied listing

Real photos, fake booking

Scammers regularly lift genuine hotel photos and reviews to dress up a fake listing or a cloned website that looks convincing at a glance.

The reporting system changed

Action Fraud has become Report Fraud

Action Fraud closed and was replaced by a new service called Report Fraud, run by the same City of London Police team, with the changeover completed by January 2026.

£11 million lost to holiday scams, and sale season is one of the riskiest times

Which? reported in August 2026 that holiday scams stole £11 million from Brits in 2024 alone, across more than 6,000 reports, an average loss of £1,844 per victim. That's not a new problem either. Action Fraud, before it became Report Fraud, logged 6,640 reports of holiday fraud in 2023, with victims losing a combined £12.3 million, an average of £1,851 each, and July and August were the two worst months that year for reports. ABTA's chief executive Mark Tanzer has made the same point every year since: fraudsters target popular destinations and busy times of year, and victims often only discover the fraud just before they travel or while they're already away.

Black Friday and the January sales rush add another peak on top of the usual summer one, because the entire pitch of a flash sale is urgency, which is exactly the environment a fake deal thrives in. Which? also flagged a genuinely striking figure from NordVPN's own research: during February's peak booking period, NordVPN found around 40 new fraudulent Booking.com websites appearing every single day, with Airbnb the second most impersonated travel brand. AI is making these cloned sites noticeably more convincing than they used to be.

How the fake listings and cloned websites actually work

Which?'s research points to a handful of genuinely recurring patterns, rather than one single type of scam.

  • 1Cloned comparison or booking websites. Built to look nearly identical to a real travel company, often with a web address that's only subtly different, an extra word, a misspelling, or an unusual character.
  • 2Fake accommodation listings on real platforms. Scammers post listings using photos and reviews copied from a genuine property. Which? even tested this itself, successfully listing a holiday home on Booking.com without the platform verifying its identity first.
  • 3Fake messages from a "host" or "hotel". Sent by email, text, phone or WhatsApp, asking you to verify payment details or share personal data to secure a booking, often outside the platform you actually booked through.
  • 4Fake social media profiles and adverts. New accounts, or accounts with names or locations unrelated to the real company, often pushing a "limited time only" discount code or a competition that mimics a well known brand.
!
Jake's top tip

Reverse image search any accommodation photos you're not sure about using Google Images or TinEye. If the exact same photos turn up attached to a completely different property or a different country, that listing isn't genuine.

Action Fraud is gone. Report Fraud is where you report a scam now

This is the genuinely new part for this year. Action Fraud, the UK's fraud reporting service for England, Wales and Northern Ireland since 2009, has been replaced. City of London Police, who ran Action Fraud throughout, launched its successor, Report Fraud, in December 2025, with the wider public rollout continuing into January 2026. The old site was widely criticised as hard to use and slow to respond, and the new service was built with three stated aims: reduce the burden on victims, give clearer advice and reassurance, and generate richer intelligence that every police force can actually access.

The practical details that matter if you ever need to use it: report online at reportfraud.police.uk, the old actionfraud.police.uk address now redirects there automatically. The phone number is unchanged, 0300 123 2040. You'll still get a crime reference number and updates by email or post, and you can still report fraud on behalf of someone else. Scotland isn't covered by the new service yet and is expected to join sometime in 2026, so for now people in Scotland should keep reporting to Police Scotland on 101. A suspicious website itself is also worth reporting separately to the National Cyber Security Centre.

The checks that actually catch a fake holiday deal

None of these take more than a couple of minutes, and together they catch the vast majority of fake deals before any money changes hands.

  • 1Check ABTA membership directly. Use the member search tool at abta.com rather than trusting a logo on the page itself, which can simply be copied onto a fake site.
  • 2Check ATOL protection directly. Search by company name or ATOL number at atol.org/check-for-atol. A genuine ATOL comes with a four or five digit number, which sometimes starts with a T, and the search confirms it's the one actually being advertised.
  • 3Pay by credit card wherever you can. It gives you added protection under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act for purchases over £100 and up to £30,000, protection a bank transfer or debit card doesn't automatically give you. Be especially wary of being asked to pay a private individual's bank account directly.
  • 4Look properly at the website itself. Check the web address for unusual wording or characters, use a domain checker like who.is to see how recently it was created, and read the most recent reviews rather than just the overall star rating.
  • 5Treat urgency as a warning, not a bargain. Check any "exclusive" offer directly with the company it claims to be from before paying anything, especially around Black Friday when pressure tactics are everywhere.

What this actually means before you book this Black Friday

A good Black Friday holiday deal genuinely exists most years. The only real shift needed is treating an unusually cheap price as a reason to check a little further, rather than a reason to rush.

  • 1Check ABTA membership and ATOL protection directly at abta.com and atol.org before paying anything, never just from a logo on the page.
  • 2Pay by credit card wherever possible, for the added Section 75 protection it gives you.
  • 3Reverse image search accommodation photos and check how new a website is if anything feels slightly off.
  • 4Be more suspicious, not less, the bigger the discount. A genuinely huge saving is rare. A fake one claiming to be huge is common.
  • 5Know where to report it now. Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk, 0300 123 2040) has replaced Action Fraud, and a suspicious website itself can also be reported to the National Cyber Security Centre.

Quick answers

How much is actually lost to holiday scams each year?

Which? reported in August 2026 that holiday scams stole £11 million in 2024, across more than 6,000 reports, an average of £1,844 per victim. The year before, Action Fraud logged 6,640 reports and £12.3 million lost, an average of £1,851 each, with July and August the worst months. Black Friday and the January sales add another spike on top of the usual summer peak.

What happened to Action Fraud, and where do I report a scam now?

Action Fraud has been replaced by a new service called Report Fraud, run by the same City of London Police team, launched in December 2025 with the wider rollout continuing into January 2026. Report online at reportfraud.police.uk, which the old actionfraud.police.uk address now redirects to automatically, or call 0300 123 2040, the number hasn't changed. Scotland is expected to join the new service sometime in 2026 and should keep reporting to Police Scotland on 101 until then.

How do I check a travel company is genuinely ABTA and ATOL protected?

Use ABTA's own member search tool at abta.com, and check ATOL protection directly at atol.org/check-for-atol by searching the company name or ATOL number. Never rely on a logo displayed on the company's own website, since that can simply be copied onto a fake site.

What's the safest way to pay for a holiday?

By credit card wherever possible, since it gives you added protection under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act for purchases over £100 and up to £30,000, protection a bank transfer or debit card doesn't automatically offer. Be especially cautious if you're ever asked to pay into a private individual's bank account.

What should actually make me suspicious of a Black Friday holiday deal?

A price that's noticeably cheaper than everywhere else for the exact same hotel and dates, pressure to book immediately, a website that's very new or has a slightly unusual address, and accommodation photos that turn up elsewhere when reverse searched. Any one of these is worth checking further before you pay.
