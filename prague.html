PRAGUE

Charles Bridge, the Castle, cheap beer and a proper fairytale skyline, weather by month, where to stay, things to do and what it actually costs, everything you need to plan a city break to Prague.

What Jake says about Prague

Prague is one of the easiest city breaks I sell. It's a short flight, it's genuinely stunning to look at, and your money goes further than almost anywhere else in Europe. Clients who've done the usual Paris or Amsterdam weekend and want somewhere a bit different, with better value food and drink, almost always come back raving about it.

Prague is a popular city break, with flights and a hotel put together as one package, and below I can point to specific, real hotels with confirmed availability.

!
Jake's top tip

Prague is also one of the most popular stag and hen destinations I get asked about. I'm upfront with clients either way: it's a fantastic sightseeing city break for couples and families too, it just also happens to have a big nightlife scene if that's what a group is after.

Trip length & who it suits

Three to four nights is the sweet spot for a first visit, long enough to cover the Castle, Old Town and Charles Bridge properly without rushing, and still feel like a proper break rather than a flying visit. Five nights gives room to add a day trip out to Kutna Hora or Cesky Krumlov as well. Anything shorter than three nights feels like hard work given the walking involved, and there's rarely a need to stay much longer than five or six nights since the historic centre is fairly compact.

Prague suits couples, groups of friends, city break regulars and families with older children who'll enjoy the castle and the clock tower. It's also a genuinely popular stag and hen destination thanks to its nightlife and low prices. It's less suited to clients wanting total relaxation, since it's a walking city with a lot of cobbles, or to anyone travelling with a pram or with limited mobility, who should budget extra time and stick closer to the metro and tram network.

Getting there

Direct flights from the UK take around 1 hour 40 minutes to 2 hours depending on the departure airport, one of the shortest flight times of any city break I sell. Flights run from a good spread of UK airports, so there's strong choice and competition on price, and it's an easy short haul booking even for a long weekend.

!
Jake's top tip

Because the flight is so short, I often suggest Prague to clients who are nervous about flying or who just want a city break that doesn't eat into the weekend with a long journey either end.

Best time to visit

April to May and September to October are my pick for most clients, mild temperatures, decent sunshine and noticeably fewer crowds than peak summer. June to August is warmest and liveliest, but also the busiest and most expensive, with the most rainy days of the year despite the heat. November through February is a different trip entirely: proper winter cold, but also Prague's Christmas markets, which run from late November into early January and are some of the best in Europe.

Jake's top tip

If a client is specifically after the Christmas markets, I point them to my full comparison of Budapest, Vienna and Prague’s Christmas markets, Prague works out the cheapest of the three and has the shortest flight time.

Where to stay

Prague's historic centre is compact, so most areas worth staying in are within walking distance of each other. Which one you pick changes the feel of the trip.

Old Town & Josefov

The historic heart of the city, with Old Town Square, the Astronomical Clock and the Jewish Quarter all on your doorstep. The most atmospheric base, and also the busiest and priciest.

New Town & Wenceslas Square

Just south of Old Town, with excellent metro and tram links, a wider choice of hotels, and generally better value than staying right in the historic core. A strong all round pick.

Castle District & Malá Strana

Quieter and more upscale, across the river from the main sights, with colourful baroque streets and some of the best views in the city. Expect steeper, cobbled walking and a short tram ride into the thick of things.

Vinohrady & Žižkov

A more residential, local feel with good independent restaurants and bars, a short tram or metro ride from the centre. Usually the best value option, popular with clients who've done the main sights before.

!
Jake's top tip

Not sure which to recommend? Old Town for a first time visitor who wants everything on their doorstep, New Town for the best balance of location and value, and Vinohrady for anyone wanting a quieter, more local feel at a lower price.

Getting around

Prague's integrated public transport network covers the metro's three lines, trams and buses on one ticket, bought as a 30 minute, 90 minute, 24 hour or 72 hour pass from the PID Lítačka app, yellow machines at metro stations, or a card reader on board. It's one of the cheapest, easiest systems in Europe, and most visitors won't need anything else, the historic centre is also very walkable, though cobbles and occasional steep streets around the Castle are worth factoring in for anyone with mobility needs. Bolt and Uber both operate widely for anything further out or late at night. From the airport, bus 119 or 100 connects to the metro, or the Airport Express bus runs direct to the main train station, both taking around 35 to 45 minutes; taxis and Bolt cover the same journey in 20 to 30 minutes depending on traffic.

!
Jake's top tip

I always tell clients to buy transport tickets through the official PID Lítačka app rather than from a stranger on the street near Old Town Square, a well known scam involves overpriced or fake tickets sold just outside tourist hotspots.

Places to visit

Prague Castle & St Vitus Cathedral

The largest ancient castle complex in the world, home to St Vitus Cathedral, and worth a half day on its own. Arrive early to beat the coach tour crowds, especially in summer.

Charles Bridge & Old Town Square

Free to walk any time, best early morning before the crowds arrive. Back in Old Town Square, the Astronomical Clock on the Old Town Hall puts on an hourly show from 9am to 11pm.

The Jewish Quarter (Josefov)

A cluster of historic synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery, tucked between Old Town Square and the river. A moving, worthwhile couple of hours, and well placed for anyone staying in Old Town.

Petřín Hill & Lookout Tower

A short funicular ride up to a mini Eiffel Tower lookalike, with some of the best views in the city and far fewer crowds than the Castle. A relaxed way to spend a couple of hours, and good value if a client already holds a transport pass.

!
Jake's top tip

A lot of clients assume the euro is accepted everywhere since it's an EU country, but the Czech Republic has its own currency, the koruna. Euros are sometimes taken in very touristy spots at a poor exchange rate, so I always tell clients to withdraw or pay in koruna.
