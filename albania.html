ALBANIA: THE ALBANIAN RIVIERA

Turquoise bays that genuinely rival Greece, mountains running straight down to the sea, and prices that still feel like a decade ago. Albania is the destination clients ask me about more than almost anywhere else right now. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to the Albanian Riviera, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about the Albanian Riviera

I get asked about Albania constantly at the moment, and once clients see the photos I understand why. The Albanian Riviera, the stretch of coast running from Vlore down to Saranda, has the same turquoise water and dramatic mountains as the Greek islands next door, at a fraction of the price, and it still feels properly undiscovered compared to where I usually book. Saranda is the main hub, a lively seafront town that makes a sensible base, with Ksamil's white sand islands, the Blue Eye spring and the ancient ruins at Butrint all within easy reach.

Jake's top tip

Albania is still a genuine value destination, so I always set expectations that it is also still developing in places. Some stretches of the coast have building work going on alongside finished hotels, which is part of why it is such good value right now.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven to ten nights on the Riviera works well, long enough for Saranda, a few days in Ksamil or Dhermi, and day trips to Butrint and the Blue Eye without feeling rushed. Many of my clients add on Tirana, Corfu or further up the coast too, which I cover below.

It suits clients who love the look of Greece but want better value, couples and groups after somewhere that still feels a little off the beaten track, and anyone happy to embrace a destination that is developing fast rather than fully finished. It is less suited to clients who want five star resort infrastructure everywhere they look, since that is still patchy outside the main hotels, or anyone who wants to fly direct into the resort itself, since the nearest airport is Tirana, a transfer away from the coast.

Getting there

There are direct flights to Tirana (TIA) from nine UK airports: British Airways from London Heathrow, Wizz Air from London Gatwick and Luton, and Ryanair from Birmingham, Edinburgh, Leeds Bradford, Liverpool, Manchester and London Stansted. Flight time is around three hours.

Tirana is not on the coast though, so getting to the Riviera itself means a further transfer, around five to six hours by coach or private car down to Saranda. A route I often suggest instead is flying into Corfu, which has more frequent and often cheaper flights from the UK in summer, then taking the ferry across to Saranda in as little as thirty minutes. I can help work out which route suits your dates best.

Jake's top tip

The Corfu ferry route catches a lot of clients out when they book flights without checking first, so I always confirm the full journey, not just the flight, before anyone books.

Jake's top tip

Sea temperatures reach their warmest, around 24 to 25 degrees, in August and September, so September gives you that warm sea with noticeably fewer crowds than July or August.

Best time to visit

June and September are my favourite months to recommend, with warm, swimmable sea, reliably sunny weather and noticeably fewer crowds and lower prices than peak summer. July and August are the hottest and busiest months, and also when prices and crowds both peak, so I always book those well ahead if clients want those specific dates. May is a good value shoulder month too, though the sea takes a little longer to warm up. Over winter, from November to March, much of the Riviera's beach infrastructure closes down, so I tend to steer winter clients towards a city break in Tirana instead.

Where to stay on the Albanian Riviera

Four real, bookable picks around Saranda and Dhermi.

Best value

Hotel Seaside Saranda

A straightforward, well located hotel just two minutes' walk from Mango Beach, with triple and quad rooms that suit families well and a terrace restaurant looking out over the sea. A sensible, affordable base for a first trip. I can price this one up for you.

Best boutique & design

La Brisa Boutique Hotel, Dhermi

A sleek, contemporary hotel right on the beach in Dhermi's old village, with a rooftop infinity pool looking out over the Ionian Sea and a beach bar with sand underfoot. A lovely choice for couples who want style as well as sea views. I can price this one up for you.

Best family & resort feel

Mango Luxury Resorts, Saranda

Steps from Mango Beach with a private beach area, two restaurants, evening shows and rooms ranging from doubles to family suites with their own living areas. A great fit for families who want everything close by. I can price this one up for you.

Best spa & sea views

Bougainville Bay Resort & Spa, Saranda

A smart hotel close to Saranda's centre with indoor and outdoor pools, a spa with a sauna and steam room, and family suites and duplexes with sea views. A good choice for anyone who wants a proper spa alongside the beach. I can price this one up for you.

Facilities as published at the time of writing. I always check current pricing, board basis and availability before booking.

Getting around

There is no railway on the Riviera, so a hire car is the easiest way to properly explore the coast road between Saranda, Ksamil, Himare and Dhermi at your own pace, and it is one of the most scenic drives in Europe. Shared minibuses called furgons run between the main towns and are very cheap, though timetables are informal, so I would not rely on them for anything time sensitive. Taxis in and around Saranda are plentiful and inexpensive, and I can arrange private transfers for arrivals, departures and day trips if you would rather not drive.

Places to visit

Butrint

A UNESCO World Heritage site a short drive from Saranda, with layers of Greek, Roman and Venetian ruins set in a quiet lagoon nature reserve. One of the best value sightseeing days on the whole coast.

Ksamil

Often called the Albanian Maldives, with white sand beaches and a scatter of small islands just offshore that are easy to reach by kayak or a short boat hop. Busy in peak summer, so I always suggest an early start.

Gjirokaster

A UNESCO listed old town of stone Ottoman houses climbing a hillside below a hilltop castle, about an hour inland from Saranda. Easily combined with Blue Eye on a day trip.

The Llogara Pass & Porto Palermo

The coast road north of Himare climbs dramatically through the Llogara Pass before dropping back to the sea at Porto Palermo, home to a small 19th century castle on its own peninsula. One of the best drives on the Riviera.

Multi centre: combining the Riviera with somewhere else

Albania does not come as one simple flight and hotel package in the way some of my other destinations do, so I usually build it as a tailor made trip, and that makes it easy to add somewhere else on. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

The Riviera & Tirana

Add two or three nights in the capital at the start or end of the trip, for museums, Bunk'Art and the old bazaar, staying at the five star Rogner Hotel Tirana right in the centre. About five to six hours from Saranda by coach or private transfer.

The Riviera & Corfu

The easiest add on of the three. Take the ferry across to Corfu in as little as thirty minutes and finish the trip at Kontokali Bay Resort & Spa, a genuine twin centre a lot of my clients enjoy for the contrast between the two coastlines.

The Riviera & Lake Ohrid, North Macedonia

For something further off the beaten track, continue on from Tirana to Lake Ohrid, about two and a half to three hours away, and stay at the lakefront Hotel Metropol Lake Resort. A beautiful, little visited corner of the Balkans.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.
