JAPAN

Tokyo's neon streets, Mount Fuji, a bullet train route through Takayama, Kanazawa, Hiroshima and Kyoto. The one genuine way I can currently book Japan, exactly how it works, what it costs and what to expect.

What Jake says about Japan

Japan comes up in conversation more than almost any other long haul destination I get asked about, but it's also one of the more unusual ones for me to book. Unlike most of what I sell, there's no simple flight and hotel package to a single resort or city. TUI is currently the only one of my three operators, TUI, Jet2holidays and easyJet holidays, that sells Japan at all, since neither Jet2holidays nor easyJet holidays flies long haul yet. And even through TUI, Japan isn't sold as a stay in one place, it's a single fully escorted tour called Discover Japan.

It's a proper 12 night route through six stops, Tokyo, Hakone, Takayama, Kanazawa, Hiroshima and Kyoto, with flights, hotels, transfers, a local English speaking guide and a run of included experiences all built in. It isn't cheap, current prices start from around £5,127 per person, but everything from the flights down to the bullet train tickets is arranged for you, which a lot of clients find genuinely reassuring for a first trip somewhere this different.

!
Jake's top tip

I always set expectations early that this is the one Japan option I can currently book: a single escorted tour, not a pick your own hotel package. If you want a fully independent, build it yourself Japan itinerary, that's a different conversation, and not something I can book through TUI, Jet2holidays or easyJet holidays.

Trip length & who it suits

The tour runs 12 nights, 14 days including the two flight days, and TUI rates it as an active pace tour, meaning early starts, some long days on the bullet train or coach, and a fair amount of walking at each stop. There's a minimum tour age of 16 and a maximum group size of 25. It suits couples, friends travelling together, honeymooners after something different from the usual beach week, and anyone who has always wanted to properly see Japan rather than just tick off Tokyo. It's less suited to young families given the minimum age, to anyone wanting a slower, more relaxing pace, or to anyone who only wants a few days in Tokyo itself, since this tour covers the whole route in one booking.

Getting there

TUI flies into Tokyo Narita International Airport from a choice of UK departure airports, including Manchester, Gatwick, Heathrow, Edinburgh and Glasgow, with the exact routing, direct or via a connecting hub, depending on your chosen airport and date. Flight time is upwards of 12 hours even on a direct service, more on an indirect one, so this is very much a long haul trip. All flights, transfers, bullet train travel and coach transport between each stop on the tour are included in the TUI package price and protected under TUI's ATOL licence.

UK passport holders currently travel to Japan visa free for tourist stays of up to 90 days, with no advance paperwork needed beyond a passport valid for the length of your stay. Japan has announced a new pre travel digital screening system called JESTA, broadly similar to Europe's ETIAS scheme, but based on current reporting it isn't expected to become mandatory before 2028 at the earliest, so it won't affect a booking made for the tour's current April to September departures. I'll flag it here if that changes.

!
Jake's top tip

Because this is a fully escorted tour with a minimum age of 16, it isn't the holiday to book if you're travelling with younger children. For a family trip to Japan I'd need to look at a fully independent, tailor made itinerary instead, since none of my three main operators currently sell Japan any other way.

Weather by month

Tokyo has four distinct seasons: a cold, dry winter, a mild spring that brings cherry blossom, a hot and humid summer with a rainy season at its start, and a crisp fogbrooming autumn known for its colour. Figures below are for Tokyo, the natural reference point for the whole route, though Kanazawa on the tour sees noticeably more rain and snow, and Kyoto's inland position makes its summers hotter and its winters colder still.

Best time to visit

TUI currently only runs the Discover Japan tour in April, May, June and September, so it's worth being honest about what that does and doesn't line up with. April catches the tail end of cherry blossom season in most years and is the most popular, and priciest, departure month, from around £5,476 per person. May is warm and settled, a genuinely comfortable shoulder month and better value, from around £5,322 per person. June departures run into the start of the tsuyu rainy season, warmer and more humid with a higher chance of rain, but also the cheapest of the four months alongside September, from around £5,183 per person. September is TUI's lowest priced departure month, from around £5,127 per person, though it still carries typhoon season risk and the humidity of summer hasn't fully broken. There currently aren't any TUI departures in the winter months, over the autumn leaves season in November, or in July and August, so if a client's heart is set on seeing snow at the temples or the full blaze of autumn colour, I have to be upfront that this particular tour can't currently get them there.

!
Jake's top tip

With only four departure months a year and a maximum group size of 25, popular dates, especially April's cherry blossom departures, can sell out well in advance. I always suggest booking as early as possible once a date is decided.

The Discover Japan route

Because this is one escorted tour rather than a pick your own hotel holiday, where you stay is set by the itinerary. Here's how the 12 nights break down across the six stops.

Tokyo

Two nights at the start of the tour and a final night at the end before flying home, with a city tour taking in Asakusa's Sensoji Temple and the Meiji Jingu shrine. Hotel standard is 3 or 4 star, chosen by TUI for location rather than named in advance.

Hakone & Mount Fuji

One night at Hakone, with views of Mount Fuji and a boat trip on Lake Ashi, before the tour moves on by bullet train. Same 3 to 4 star hotel standard throughout.

Takayama & Shirakawago

Two nights in the beautifully preserved old town of Takayama, with walking tours there and at the thatched roof village of Shirakawago, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Kanazawa

One night in Kanazawa, with a bus tour taking in Kenrokuen, rated as one of Japan's three great gardens, and the chance to explore the city's well preserved samurai and geisha districts.

Hiroshima & Miyajima

Two nights in Hiroshima, with a tram tour of the city, the Peace Memorial Museum and Atomic Bomb Dome, and a boat trip out to Miyajima to see the floating torii gate at Itsukushima Shrine.

Kyoto & Nara

Three nights in Kyoto, the longest single stop on the tour, including a visit to Nara to see its famous bowing deer on the way in, a Kyoto city tour, and a walk through the Arashiyama bamboo grove.

TUI doesn't name individual hotels in advance for this tour, only that they're a mix of 3 and 4 star standard across the six stops, with breakfast included throughout plus a handful of extra lunches and dinners built into the included experiences. I'll confirm exact hotels with you once a specific departure date is booked.

!
Jake's top tip

Your main luggage doesn't travel with you on the bullet train between stops. TUI's tour uses Japan's Takkyubin door to door delivery service instead, at roughly £13 to £16 per case, arranged by your tour guide. Worth packing a smaller overnight bag for the train travel days themselves.

Getting around

Everything on this tour, bullet train travel, coaches and transfers between each of the six stops, is arranged and included as part of the TUI package, with a local English speaking guide throughout, so there's genuinely nothing to organise yourself once you've booked. If you extend your own time in Tokyo or Kyoto before or after the tour, Japan's public transport is some of the most reliable in the world: an IC card such as Suica or Pasmo covers trains, subways and buses in every city on the route, tapping in and out much like an Oyster card, and also works for small purchases in convenience stores and vending machines.

Places to visit

teamLab Planets, Tokyo

An immersive digital art museum in Toyosu where you walk barefoot through water and mirrored rooms of shifting light and colour. A genuinely different few hours, and a good one to add on around the tour's Tokyo nights.

Sensoji Temple, Asakusa

Tokyo's oldest temple, approached through the Kaminarimon gate and a long street of traditional shops. Included on the tour's own Tokyo city stop, and worth a second visit in the evening when it's quieter.

Nara's deer park

Over a thousand wild sika deer roam freely around Nara's temples and are used to being hand fed special crackers sold nearby. A short stop built into the tour's journey from Hiroshima to Kyoto.

Kiyomizu-dera, Kyoto

A UNESCO listed wooden temple on a hillside above Kyoto, with a famous wooden stage and sweeping views over the city. Not on the tour's set itinerary, but an easy add on during the three nights in Kyoto.
