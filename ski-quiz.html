The full resort guide

Not sure which ski resort is right for you? Here's the short version.

Five ways people actually ski, and five resorts Jake rates for each, from the biggest, snow-sure ski areas to the best-value resorts for a proper week on snow without the big price tag. Swipe through each row, or take the quiz above for a personal match.

The Piste Chaser: You're here to ski. All of it.

You measure a trip in vertical metres, not lie-ins. Big, joined-up terrain matters more to you than nightlife or spa days, and you want to ski every corner of the map before the lifts shut.

Val Thorens, France

Splurge

The highest resort in Europe, and part of the Three Valleys, the biggest linked ski area there is. You will run out of daylight before you run out of pistes.

11 green · 29 blue · 29 red · 9 black

  • 150km of piste, 29 lifts
  • 2300m to 3230m altitude range
  • Best for: Snow-sure high-altitude skiing, groups of friends and lively apres-ski

Off the slopes:

  • Aquamotion aquatic centre: slides, wave pool, spa and Nordic baths
  • La Bee toboggan/luge run and the illuminated ice grotto
  • Snowmobile safaris and dog-sledding excursions above the resort
Ask Jake about Val Thorens, France

Whistler Blackcomb, Canada

Splurge

Two enormous mountains joined by a gondola, with more marked runs than most people could ski in a fortnight.

40 green · 110 blue · 0 red · 50 black

  • 200km of piste, 37 lifts
  • 675m to 2284m altitude range
  • Best for: Groups of friends, expert/off-piste skiers and anyone wanting North America's biggest resort

Off the slopes:

  • Scandinave Spa Whistler outdoor hydrotherapy circuit
  • Vallea Lumina night-time light and sound forest walk
  • PEAK 2 PEAK Gondola ride between Whistler and Blackcomb summits, plus the Whistler Village Stroll for shopping and nightlife
Ask Jake about Whistler Blackcomb, Canada

Sölden, Austria

Mid-range

Big, snow-sure and joined across two glaciers, ideal if you want to clock serious mileage without worrying about the snow running out.

20 green · 30 blue · 22 red · 3 black

  • 146km of piste, 31 lifts
  • 1350m to 3340m altitude range
  • Best for: Confident intermediates, groups of friends and glacier skiing early/late season

Off the slopes:

  • 007 Elements James Bond cinematic installation on the Gaislachkogl summit
  • Aqua Dome thermal spa and sauna world in nearby Längenfeld
  • Ice Q restaurant with panoramic glacier views (as seen in Spectre)
Ask Jake about Sölden, Austria

Les Deux Alpes, France

Mid-range

One of the highest lift-served glaciers in Europe, so you can ski right through summer conditions and rack up serious vertical without worrying about the snow holding up.

25 green · 25 blue · 30 red · 15 black

  • 220km of piste, 40 lifts
  • 1280m to 3523m altitude range
  • Best for: Groups of friends, snowboarders/freestylers and nightlife-seekers

Off the slopes:

  • Toboggan/luge run and the Diable au Coeur mountain-top restaurant zipline area
  • La Chalcedoine spa and wellness centre in the village
  • Summer glacier snowboard/freestyle park and the resort's lively main-street nightlife strip
Ask Jake about Les Deux Alpes, France

Bansko, Bulgaria

Budget-friendly

Bulgaria's biggest ski area, and proof that big mileage doesn't have to mean a big price tag. Wide, well-groomed pistes and lift queues you can actually live with.

8 green · 13 blue · 5 red · 2 black

  • 75km of piste, 14 lifts
  • 990m to 2560m altitude range
  • Best for: Budget-conscious groups, beginners and families

Off the slopes:

  • Bansko's cobbled old town Pirin Street: bars, mehanas (taverns) and nightlife
  • Thermal mineral spa pools at nearby Banya village
  • Snowmobile tours and horse-drawn sleigh rides through the pine forests
Ask Jake about Bansko, Bulgaria

The Powder Hunter: The piste map is just a suggestion.

Marked runs are where you start the day, not where you finish it. You're happiest off-piste, trying something new, and coming home with a story that starts with 'so this was a bit terrifying...'

Chamonix, France

Splurge

Legendary off-piste at the foot of Mont Blanc, with guiding and touring options for pushing past the marked runs.

16 green · 27 blue · 25 red · 12 black

  • 151km of piste, 49 lifts
  • 1035m to 3842m altitude range
  • Best for: Expert and off-piste/freeride skiers, mountaineers

Off the slopes:

  • Aiguille du Midi cable car and the glass-floored 'Step into the Void' skywalk
  • Off-piste Vallée Blanche glacier descent with a mountain guide
  • Chamonix's Rue du Docteur Paccard: restaurants, bars and mountaineering shops
Ask Jake about Chamonix, France

Verbier, Switzerland

Splurge

Some of the best freeride terrain in the Alps, plus heli-skiing if you want to go all in.

20 green · 55 blue · 45 red · 12 black

  • 412km of piste, 68 lifts
  • 1350m to 3330m altitude range
  • Best for: Expert/off-piste skiers and groups of friends who like nightlife

Off the slopes:

  • Farinet Lounge/Farm Club apres-ski and nightlife scene
  • Freeride skiing off Mont-Fort and Mont-Gelé (birthplace of the Freeride World Tour)
  • Verbier Sport Plus wellness centre with pool, spa and ice rink
Ask Jake about Verbier, Switzerland

Niseko, Japan

Splurge

Famous for the lightest, deepest powder going, and a backcountry scene that draws serious skiers from all over the world.

15 green · 13 blue · 6 red · 4 black

  • 51km of piste, 32 lifts
  • 255m to 1188m altitude range
  • Best for: Powder-hunters, groups of friends and Japanese-culture-curious couples

Off the slopes:

  • Onsen (hot spring) bathing, e.g. Yukoro Onsen or the resort's hotel onsens
  • Night skiing under floodlights across Grand Hirafu and Hanazono
  • Backcountry/tree-run powder skiing and Niseko Village's izakaya restaurant strip
Ask Jake about Niseko, Japan

Passo Tonale, Italy

Mid-range

Wide open bowls, a glacier at the top, and some of the best-value off-piste access in the Alps. Less crowded than the big names, with terrain that rewards exploring.

10 green · 25 blue · 20 red · 5 black

  • 100km of piste, 29 lifts
  • 1121m to 3000m altitude range
  • Best for: Families and beginners/early intermediates

Off the slopes:

  • Presena Glacier skiing and the Passo Tonale WWI open-air museum trail
  • Family-friendly toboggan run and snow-tubing park
  • Spa and wellness centre at one of the resort's hotels, plus the compact village's ski-in/ski-out nightlife strip
Ask Jake about Passo Tonale, Italy

Jasná, Slovakia

Budget-friendly

The Tatras' best-kept secret. Genuinely varied terrain and proper backcountry pockets, at a fraction of what you'd pay for the same adventure further west.

10 green · 12 blue · 8 red · 1 black

  • 52km of piste, 20 lifts
  • 943m to 2004m altitude range
  • Best for: Families and budget-conscious groups/beginners

Off the slopes:

  • Aquapark Tatralandia water and thermal park in nearby Liptovský Mikuláš
  • Freeride zones and off-piste bowls beneath Chopok's summit
  • Demänovská Cave of Liberty ice/cave system and the resort's mountain-hut restaurants
Ask Jake about Jasná, Slovakia

The Après Legend: Skiing is just the warm-up.

You're here for the whole holiday, not just the mountain, and for you that means the bars, the bands, and the walk of shame back to the hotel still in your ski boots.

St Anton am Arlberg, Austria

Splurge

The spiritual home of après-ski. The parties start on the mountain and don't really stop.

8 green · 25 blue · 35 red · 12 black

  • 130km of piste, 41 lifts
  • 1300m to 2811m altitude range
  • Best for: Expert/off-piste skiers and groups of friends who like a big apres-ski scene

Off the slopes:

  • MooserWirt and Krazy Kanguruh: Europe's most famous apres-ski bars
  • Off-piste ski touring and the legendary Valluga freeride terrain
  • Arlberg-well.com spa and wellness centre in the village
Ask Jake about St Anton am Arlberg, Austria

Mayrhofen, Austria

Mid-range

A proper party resort with a young, lively crowd and bars that get going the second the lifts close.

12 green · 22 blue · 27 red · 3 black

  • 139km of piste, 58 lifts
  • 630m to 2500m altitude range
  • Best for: Groups of friends/apres-ski crowds and intermediates

Off the slopes:

  • The Harakiri piste: one of the steepest marked runs in the Alps (78% gradient)
  • Europahaus Mayrhofen spa, pool and wellness centre
  • Snowbombing festival atmosphere and the village's busy apres-ski bars
Ask Jake about Mayrhofen, Austria

Ischgl, Austria

Splurge

Big-name concerts, big nights out, and skiing across the Austria-Switzerland border by day.

5 green · 14 blue · 42 red · 13 black

  • 200km of piste, 42 lifts
  • 1380m to 2900m altitude range
  • Best for: Intermediates, groups of friends and apres-ski/nightlife fans

Off the slopes:

  • Ischgl's famous season-opening/closing concerts and Kuhstall/Trofana Alm apres-ski bars
  • Cross-border 'ski to Samnaun' run into Switzerland for duty-free shopping
  • Silvretta Therme Ischgl spa and thermal baths
Ask Jake about Ischgl, Austria

Saalbach Hinterglemm, Austria

Mid-range

One giant, joined-up party circuit disguised as a ski area. The bars start filling from early afternoon and the whole valley is built for a big night.

6 green · 31 blue · 27 red · 6 black

  • 200km of piste, 52 lifts
  • 1000m to 2020m altitude range
  • Best for: Groups of friends, intermediates and apres-ski/nightlife fans

Off the slopes:

  • Ski Circus round trip linking Saalbach, Hinterglemm, Leogang and Fieberbrunn
  • Baumbar Lounge and the Saalbach 'apres-ski mile' nightlife strip
  • Alpine Coaster toboggan run and the Panorama Park scenic hiking/sledding trails
Ask Jake about Saalbach Hinterglemm, Austria

Borovets, Bulgaria

Budget-friendly

Cheap drinks, a lively strip, and a crowd that's there for a good time as much as the skiing. Your money goes a long way here, on and off the mountain.

3 green · 11 blue · 9 red · 4 black

  • 58km of piste, 11 lifts
  • 1320m to 2550m altitude range
  • Best for: Budget-conscious groups of friends and beginners/families

Off the slopes:

  • Borovets' lively apres-ski strip of bars and clubs at the resort centre
  • Snowmobile safaris and quad-biking excursions in the Rila mountains
  • Horse-drawn sleigh rides through the pine forest to mountain hut restaurants
Ask Jake about Borovets, Bulgaria

The Slow Skier: It's a holiday. Act like it.

You'll ski, but you're not in a hurry, and you're definitely not missing a long lunch for one more run. Good food, a proper spa, and views worth stopping for matter just as much as the skiing.

Courchevel, France

Splurge

Effortlessly smart, with the kind of restaurants and hotels that make lounging around feel entirely justified.

27 green · 40 blue · 42 red · 10 black

  • 150km of piste, 67 lifts
  • 1300m to 2740m altitude range
  • Best for: Luxury travellers, families and couples wanting a five-star experience

Off the slopes:

  • Luxury boutique shopping street in Courchevel 1850 (Rue de l'Eglise)
  • Aquamotion water park (shared with Val Thorens' network) and Courchevel's own spas/Michelin-starred restaurants
  • Ice rink, dog-sledding and paragliding above the resort
Ask Jake about Courchevel, France

Lech am Arlberg, Austria

Splurge

Quietly luxurious, beautifully kept, and about as relaxed as skiing gets.

4 green · 42 blue · 40 red · 18 black

  • 110km of piste, 34 lifts
  • 1450m to 2810m altitude range
  • Best for: Couples, luxury travellers and confident intermediate/red-run skiers

Off the slopes:

  • Ski safari across the wider Arlberg lift network to Zürs and St Anton
  • Rud-Alpe and other gourmet mountain-hut restaurants (Lech is known for fine dining)
  • Vitaltherme spa in nearby Zürs/Lech village and upscale boutique shopping
Ask Jake about Lech am Arlberg, Austria

Cortina d'Ampezzo, Italy

Splurge

Dramatic Dolomites scenery, excellent food, and a pace of life that never feels rushed.

10 green · 42 blue · 19 red · 8 black

  • 120km of piste, 37 lifts
  • 1220m to 2930m altitude range
  • Best for: Couples, luxury travellers and intermediates who enjoy scenery and dining

Off the slopes:

  • Rifugio Lagazuoi and other iconic mountain-hut lunches with Dolomites views
  • Cortina's elegant Corso Italia: designer shopping and cafe culture
  • Natural ice rink (former 1956 Winter Olympics venue) and the Faloria cable car viewpoint
Ask Jake about Cortina d'Ampezzo, Italy

Alpbach, Austria

Mid-range

One of Austria's prettiest villages, chocolate-box chalets and all, with gentle, well-groomed slopes that don't ask too much of you.

5 green · 34 blue · 13 red · 18 black

  • 145km of piste, 14 lifts
  • 1000m to 2030m altitude range
  • Best for: Families and beginners/early intermediates

Off the slopes:

  • Alpbach's traditional chocolate-box village centre: widely rated one of Austria's prettiest
  • Toboggan/natural luge runs from the mountain restaurants back to the village
  • Alpbachtal Card activities including the local swimming/spa centre and ice rink
Ask Jake about Alpbach, Austria

Pamporovo, Bulgaria

Budget-friendly

Bulgaria's quietest, gentlest resort: forested, easy-going pistes and a properly relaxed pace, without the price tag of the Alps.

5 green · 4 blue · 7 red · 3 black

  • 37km of piste, 14 lifts
  • 1620m to 1931m altitude range
  • Best for: Families and beginners; a gentle, low-altitude resort

Off the slopes:

  • Wolf's Jaw (Vълчи зъб) rock formation and forest walking/sledging trails
  • Nearby Chepelare ski museum and the Perelik hotel spa/wellness centre
  • Snowmobile and horse-sledge excursions through the pine forest
Ask Jake about Pamporovo, Bulgaria

The Food-First Skier: You plan runs around restaurants, not the other way round.

Skiing's great, but let's be honest, you're really here for the food. Mountain huts, local specialities and somewhere with a proper wine list will always win over another lap of the same run.

Kitzbühel, Austria

Splurge

A genuinely beautiful old town with brilliant mountain restaurants scattered across the slopes.

10 green · 22 blue · 25 red · 13 black

  • 158km of piste, 30 lifts
  • 800m to 2000m altitude range
  • Best for: Couples, groups of friends and confident intermediate skiers

Off the slopes:

  • The Streif: the world-famous Hahnenkamm downhill race course, skiable by strong intermediates when open
  • Kitzbühel's medieval old town: boutique shopping and cafe/bar culture
  • Aquarena Kitzbühel swimming and spa complex
Ask Jake about Kitzbühel, Austria

Baqueira-Beret, Spain

Mid-range

Skiing by day, proper Spanish food and wine by night. Tapas beats a bar snack every time.

6 green · 36 blue · 29 red · 7 black

  • 160km of piste, 33 lifts
  • 1500m to 2610m altitude range
  • Best for: Groups of friends, intermediates and those wanting a Pyrenean/Spanish alternative to the Alps

Off the slopes:

  • Baqueira's renowned mountain-hut restaurants (a favourite of the Spanish royal family)
  • Aran Sky Spa wellness centre in the village
  • Off-piste and freeride bowls on the Bonaigua/Cap de Baqueira sectors
Ask Jake about Baqueira-Beret, Spain

Alta Badia, Italy

Splurge

Some of the best on-mountain dining anywhere in the Alps, deep in South Tyrol's food-obsessed corner of Italy.

8 green · 68 blue · 30 red · 5 black

  • 130km of piste, 52 lifts
  • 1324m to 2750m altitude range
  • Best for: Families, couples and intermediates who enjoy fine dining and scenery

Off the slopes:

  • Sellaronda: ski the circuit around the Sella massif linking four valleys
  • Michelin-starred mountain restaurants (Alta Badia has the highest concentration of gourmet rifugios in the Dolomites)
  • Wellness/spa centres in Corvara and La Villa, plus horse-drawn sleigh rides
Ask Jake about Alta Badia, Italy

Serre Chevalier, France

Mid-range

A string of characterful old villages linked by lift, with proper regional cooking and mountain restaurants that take food as seriously as the skiing.

25 green · 32 blue · 42 red · 15 black

  • 250km of piste, 77 lifts
  • 1350m to 2800m altitude range
  • Best for: Families, intermediates and groups wanting varied, tree-lined skiing

Off the slopes:

  • Wood-fired mountain restaurants in the pine forests around Villeneuve/Monetier
  • Thermal spa (Grands Bains du Monêtier) at the base of the resort
  • Sledging/tobogganing runs and Le Bez/Villeneuve's traditional apres-ski bars
Ask Jake about Serre Chevalier, France

Zakopane, Poland

Budget-friendly

Poland's ski capital, and the food is the whole point: hearty, cheap, and served in wooden mountain huts that feel like they haven't changed in decades.

8 green · 8 blue · 3 red · 2 black

  • 25km of piste, 25 lifts
  • 704m to 1987m altitude range
  • Best for: Beginners, families and budget-conscious groups (small, low-altitude ski areas)

Off the slopes:

  • Zakopane's Krupówki Street: Poland's best-known mountain-resort promenade for shopping, bars and street food
  • Thermal baths at Chochołowskie Termy or Bukovina Terma near Bialka Tatrzanska
  • Kasprowy Wierch cable car to the summit for views into Slovakia, and horse-drawn sleigh rides in the Tatras
Ask Jake about Zakopane, Poland
