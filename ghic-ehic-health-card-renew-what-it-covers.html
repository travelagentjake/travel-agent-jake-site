IS YOUR GHIC OR EHIC STILL VALID? WHAT THE FREE HEALTH CARD ACTUALLY COVERS ABROAD

The UK Global Health Insurance Card replaced the old EHIC at the start of 2021, and each card lasts up to five years. That means a lot of the very first GHICs, and plenty of old EHICs, are reaching their expiry date right now, just as people are booking ski trips and Christmas city breaks. Renewing is free and takes a few minutes, but there are copycat websites that will happily charge you for it. Here's exactly what the card covers, where it works, what it doesn't do, and how to renew it properly.

The short version

A UK GHIC is free and lets you get state healthcare that becomes medically necessary while you're away, at the same cost as a local resident, and sometimes for free. It works across the EU, and also in Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein. It lasts up to five years, and you can renew it up to nine months before it runs out.

It is not travel insurance. It won't pay for private hospitals, mountain rescue, treatment on a cruise ship or a flight home if you're seriously ill. The NHS and gov.uk both say plainly that you need proper travel insurance as well.

!
Jake's top tip

Get every card in the house out of the drawer tonight and check the expiry date on the front. It's printed on every card, and if yours runs out before or during your next trip, renew it now rather than the week before you fly.

Where it works, and where it doesn't

The card is designed for the EU, so Spain, Portugal, France, Italy, Greece, Cyprus, Malta, Croatia and the rest are all covered. A few others have been added since Brexit, and it's worth knowing exactly which.

All EU countriesCovered, including the Canary Islands, the Balearics, Madeira and the Greek islands
SwitzerlandCovered if you're a UK national (or a Swiss or EU citizen, refugee or stateless person, or their family member)
Norway, Iceland and LiechtensteinCovered since 1 January 2024 under a newer UK agreement, except for student cards
Turkey, Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco, Dubai, the USA and the CaribbeanNot covered at all. These aren't in the EU, so travel insurance is your only cover
AustraliaA separate UK agreement gives some necessary care, and your GHIC or EHIC is one way to prove you live in the UK

Source: gov.uk healthcare guidance for UK nationals visiting the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway or Switzerland, the NHS Business Services Authority, and gov.uk's guidance on reciprocal healthcare agreements with non-EU countries, checked at time of writing. Some older gov.uk pages still say the card can't be used in Norway, Iceland or Liechtenstein, but those predate the 2024 arrangement.

!
Jake's top tip

Turkey and Egypt are two of the most popular places I book, and neither one accepts the GHIC. Plenty of people assume it works anywhere in Europe or the Med. It doesn't, so for those two your travel insurance is doing all the work.

What it covers, and what it definitely doesn't

The card gets you state provided healthcare that becomes medically necessary during your trip and can't reasonably wait until you're home. That includes emergency treatment, visits to a state doctor or hospital, and treatment for a pre-existing or long term condition. You pay whatever a local resident would pay, which in some countries is nothing and in others is a contribution towards the bill.

Emergency and medically necessary state treatmentCovered, at the same cost as a local resident
Treatment for pre-existing and long term conditionsCovered, as long as the treatment becomes necessary during your trip
Private hospitals and private clinicsNot covered
Mountain rescue on the slopesNot covered
Treatment on board a cruise ship, even in portNot covered
Medical repatriation, a flight home if you're illNot covered
Travelling abroad specifically to get treatmentNot covered under the GHIC

The ski point matters at this time of year. Being brought down the mountain by a rescue team or helicopter is exactly the kind of cost the GHIC doesn't touch, and some resort clinics are private, which means the card won't help there either. That's why winter sports cover on your travel insurance is so important. I've written a separate guide on what to check on ski travel insurance.

!
Jake's top tip

If you're taken somewhere for treatment, ask straight away whether it's a state or private facility. If it's private, call your travel insurer's emergency line before you agree to anything, because the GHIC won't help you there.

How to renew it for free, and the copycat sites to avoid

The only official place to apply is the NHS website, and it costs nothing. You can apply for a new card up to nine months before your current one expires, and the NHS recommends applying at least two weeks before you travel so the card has time to arrive. You can add your partner and children to the same application, but each person gets their own card, so check every one.

If you still have an old EHIC, it keeps working until the expiry date printed on it. Once it runs out, you replace it with a GHIC rather than another EHIC.

The catch is the search results. Which? found unofficial sites paying for ads at the top of Google for the new card, and MoneySavingExpert has seen sites charging from around £35 to process an application that's free on the NHS site. Some have taken the money and never sent a card at all. The NHS Business Services Authority says plainly that sites charging a fee have nothing to do with it.

Forgotten or lost your card while you're away? You can apply for a Provisional Replacement Certificate, which gives you the same cover as the card itself. You can only do this once you actually need treatment during your trip, not in advance, and you can apply online or by calling the NHS Overseas Healthcare Services helpline on +44 (0)191 218 1999.

!
Jake's top tip

Type the NHS address in yourself, or go through nhs.uk, rather than clicking the first search result. If any site asks for a card payment at any point, you're in the wrong place, close it and start again.

Quick answers

Do I have to pay for a GHIC?

No. A UK GHIC is completely free from the official NHS website. Any site charging you a fee is unofficial and not connected to the NHS.

Is my old EHIC still valid?

Yes, until the expiry date printed on the front. Once it expires, you apply for a free GHIC to replace it.

When can I renew my GHIC?

Up to nine months before your current card expires. The NHS recommends applying at least two weeks before you travel so the new card arrives in time.

Does the GHIC work in Turkey or Egypt?

No. It covers the EU, plus Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein. Turkey and Egypt aren't covered, so you rely entirely on your travel insurance there.

Do I still need travel insurance if I have a GHIC?

Yes. The GHIC only covers state healthcare. It won't pay for private treatment, mountain rescue, treatment on a cruise ship or getting you home if you're seriously ill, which is exactly what travel insurance is for.
