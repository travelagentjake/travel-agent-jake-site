AGADIR

Morocco's biggest beach resort, over 300 days of sunshine a year, Morocco's best surf towns on its doorstep and the Atlas Mountains and the Sahara within easy reach. Here's exactly how I book Agadir, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Agadir

Agadir doesn't get talked about as much as Marrakech, but it's quietly one of the best value winter sun options I book. It was rebuilt almost entirely after a huge earthquake in 1960, so instead of a medina you get wide boulevards, a long sandy beach and a run of modern resort hotels, more like a classic package resort than the Morocco most people picture. It suits clients who want reliable sunshine, a proper beach and an easy flight, without the heat of the Caribbean or the crowds of the Canaries in peak season.

I put together flight and hotel packages to Agadir, covering both Agadir itself and Taghazout, the surf village just up the coast, comparing prices across the suppliers I work with.

!
Jake's top tip

A lot of clients don't realise Agadir and Taghazout are effectively the same trip, just two different bases about 20 minutes apart. Agadir gives you the long beach, the marina and the souk right outside your hotel. Taghazout gives you a quieter, surf focused village feel. I ask what matters more, choice and convenience or a slower pace, before steering someone toward either.

Trip length & who it suits

Seven nights is the shortest length I'd really suggest given the flight time, and it's enough for a straightforward beach week. Ten to fourteen nights is the sweet spot for most clients, especially anyone wanting to add on a day trip or a short stay in Marrakech or Essaouira. Agadir suits winter sun seekers, since it stays genuinely warm right through the colder UK months, surfers of every level thanks to Taghazout on its doorstep, families who want a long sandy beach and resort hotels built with kids in mind, and couples or golfers after an easy, reliable short to medium haul break.

It's less suited to clients chasing Morocco's classic imperial city culture in one trip, since Agadir itself has no medina or historic old town of its own, that side of Morocco is better found in Marrakech, which I regularly add on as a multi centre (see further down this guide). It's also not the pick for clients wanting big nightlife, Agadir is a relaxed, family friendly resort rather than a party destination.

Getting there

Flights land at Agadir Al Massira Airport (AGA), around 25 minutes from the hotel zone. Flight time from the UK runs from around 3 hours 30 minutes up to 4 hours 30 minutes depending on which airport you fly from, so it's a genuinely easy short to medium haul trip, similar in length to flying to the Canary Islands or Egypt's Red Sea coast. I compare prices across the suppliers I work with to find the best fit for your dates and departure airport.

!
Jake's top tip

I always point out to clients that Agadir's flight time is very close to the Canaries, but the weather holds up noticeably better through autumn and into early winter, so it's worth considering as an alternative if the Canaries are fully booked or the budget's tight.

Jake's top tip

Surfers chasing bigger waves should look at October to February, when swells pick up along this stretch of coast. Anyone who just wants calm, warm water for a first go on a board is better off in the May to September window.

Best time to visit

March to May and June to August both work well, giving warm, reliable weather, with spring slightly cooler and less humid than the height of summer. July and August are the hottest, driest and busiest months, when the Timitar Music Festival also takes over the streets. November to February is Agadir's low season, noticeably cheaper and quieter, and the sea stays mild enough to swim in even then, since Agadir rarely gets genuinely cold. It's also when Taghazout's surf really comes alive, with bigger swells drawing experienced surfers from October through to around February.

!
Jake's top tip

Agadir is one of the few places I sell that genuinely holds its weather through winter, so I often suggest it to clients who want a reliable sunshine break over Christmas or New Year without paying peak summer prices.

Getting around

Orange petit taxis are the easiest way to get around Agadir itself, cheap and metered, though it's worth confirming the driver is using the meter or agreeing a price before you set off. Cream coloured grand taxis cover longer routes, including out to Taghazout, usually shared with other passengers and leaving once full, or you can hire the whole taxi for a more direct, private trip. A local bus network exists but is mostly used by residents rather than tourists. Most hotels in both Agadir and Taghazout are within easy walking distance of the beach, and Taghazout itself is only around 20 to 25 minutes from central Agadir by taxi.

Places to visit

Kasbah Agadir Oufella

A 16th century hilltop fort reachable by cable car, with sweeping views over the city, the port and the beach. One of the best spots in Agadir to watch the sunset.

Souk El Had

North Africa's largest urban market, thousands of stalls selling spices, leather goods, pottery and argan oil. A proper, no frills Moroccan market rather than a tourist trap, worth setting aside a couple of hours.

Agadir Marina

A smart, modern marina lined with restaurants and cafes, with boat trips and fishing excursions departing most days. A relaxed spot for an evening out away from the main resort strip.

Agadir Beach & the Corniche

One of Morocco's longest beaches, a wide sweep of golden sand backed by a palm lined promenade, cafes and family friendly attractions. The natural heart of any stay in Agadir.

Multi centre: combining Agadir with the rest of Morocco

Agadir pairs naturally with the rest of Morocco, and I regularly build this as a proper multi centre trip rather than a single hotel stay. Here are three real ways I'd put it together.

Agadir & Marrakech

Beach time in Agadir followed by a few nights at the Savoy Le Grand Hotel, a 5 star hotel in Marrakech's Hivernage district, around 2km from the Medina and Jemaa el Fna Square. A private transfer between the two takes around 3 hours. The classic way to combine Morocco's coast with its most famous imperial city.

Agadir & Essaouira

A quieter pairing of two coastal towns, finishing the trip at the Sofitel Essaouira Mogador Golf & Spa, a 4.5 star beachfront hotel about 2km from Essaouira's walled old town, famous for its arts scene, blue fishing boats and sea breeze. Around 3 hours by road from Agadir. A good fit for clients who want more Morocco without the pace of a big city.

Agadir & the Sahara

For clients wanting the full Morocco experience, I can add on a 3 day escorted desert tour, running from Marrakech over the High Atlas Mountains to the Erg Chebbi dunes at Merzouga, taking in Ait Ben Haddou's kasbah, a camel trek and a night under canvas in a desert camp, with a transfer back to Agadir to finish the trip.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I'll put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.

Jake's top tip

Since dirhams can't be exchanged back once you're home, I always tell clients not to change up more cash than they'll realistically spend, and to use a card where they can for anything bigger.

Quick practical info

The essentials, at a glance.

CurrencyMoroccan Dirham (MAD), not freely convertible outside Morocco, so exchange on arrival and change back before you fly home
Plug typeType C and E (European two pin), voltage is 220V, a UK adaptor is needed
LanguageArabic and Tamazight (Berber) are the official languages, French is widely spoken, with some English in hotels and tourist areas
Flight time from the UKAround 3 hours 30 minutes to 4 hours 30 minutes depending on your departure airport
Time differenceThe same as the UK during British Summer Time, 1 hour ahead of the UK in winter when the UK is on GMT, with Morocco switching back to GMT for the duration of Ramadan each year
Entry requirementsNo visa needed for UK passport holders for stays of up to 90 days. Your passport needs at least 3 months left on it from your arrival date, and make sure it's stamped on arrival
