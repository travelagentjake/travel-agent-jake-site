PERU: MACHU PICCHU & THE SACRED VALLEY

A lost Inca citadel on top of a mountain, a valley of ancient terraces still farmed the same way today, and a city that still feels like the capital of an empire. Peru has been near the top of my clients' bucket lists for years, and once they book it they always say the same thing when they get back, that the photos genuinely do not do it justice. Here is exactly how I put together a trip to Machu Picchu and the Sacred Valley, the hotels I can price up for you, and what it actually costs.

What Jake says about Peru

Peru is one of those trips where the destination genuinely lives up to the hype. The classic route runs through Lima on the coast, up into Cusco and the Sacred Valley, and on to Machu Picchu itself, with the Inca terracing, the markets and the mountain scenery all adding up to one of the most rewarding trips I book. It is not a lie on a beach holiday though, there is a fair amount of altitude, travel time and early starts involved, and I always set that expectation honestly before anyone books.

I compare prices and routing across the suppliers I work with to find the best fit for your dates and budget, since this is a tailor made trip rather than a single packaged product.

!
Jake's top tip

Cusco sits at 3,400 metres above sea level, and altitude affects most people to some degree, tiredness and a mild headache are common for the first day or so. I always talk clients through pacing the first couple of days properly before they travel, and can build an extra night in the lower Sacred Valley into the itinerary first if that suits you better.

Trip length & who it suits

Ten to fourteen nights works well for the classic route, enough time for a night or two in Lima, several nights across Cusco and the Sacred Valley, an overnight near Machu Picchu itself so you are not rushing the visit, and a buffer day for altitude. Clients who want to add Lake Titicaca, the Amazon or the southern coast on top, which I cover further down, tend to go for fifteen to eighteen nights in total.

It suits clients who want a genuine bucket list trip built around history and culture, anyone happy with early starts, some walking over uneven ground and a fair amount of time on trains and internal flights, and travellers who are comfortable at altitude or willing to pace themselves if they are not sure. It is less suited to anyone with very limited mobility, since Machu Picchu itself involves steps and slopes with no lift access, or to clients who want a short break, since the flight time alone makes this a trip of two weeks or more to feel worthwhile.

Getting there

There are no direct flights from the UK to Lima. The usual routes connect through Madrid with Iberia, Amsterdam with KLM, or through a US gateway such as Miami or Atlanta, with a total journey time of around fifteen to eighteen hours depending on the connection. From Lima, Cusco is a further internal flight of around one hour fifteen minutes, there is no practical overland alternative given the distance and terrain.

I always try to book morning flights for the Lima to Cusco leg specifically, since afternoon flights into Cusco are more prone to weather related delays and cancellations because of winds picking up around the airport later in the day.

!
Jake's top tip

I build in a spare night in Lima before the flight to Cusco wherever possible, so a delayed or cancelled connection does not put the rest of the trip at risk. It has saved more than one itinerary.

Jake's top tip

Nights are genuinely cold across the dry season, June and July especially, even though the days are warm and sunny. Packing proper layers for the evenings catches a lot of first time visitors out.

Best time to visit

May to September is the dry season and the classic time to go, with the clearest skies, the best trekking conditions and the lowest chance of a washed out visit to Machu Picchu. July and August are also the busiest and most expensive months, booked up well in advance for the Inca Trail especially. April and late October into November are good shoulder months, with thinner crowds, lower prices and still largely decent weather, which is where I point a lot of my clients. December to March is the wet season, and February in particular sees the Inca Trail itself close completely for annual maintenance, though Machu Picchu is still reachable by train throughout.

Where to stay

Four real, bookable picks across the classic Cusco, Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu circuit.

Best value, Cusco

Andenes al Cielo, Cusco

A colonial mansion a few blocks from the Plaza de Armas, with heated rooms, breakfast included and a rooftop terrace looking out over the city's red tiled roofs. A sensible, well located base for a first night or two acclimatising. I can price this one up for you.

Best for the valley itself

La Casona de Yucay, Sacred Valley

A historic house dating to 1810, once used to shelter Simon Bolivar, with 56 rooms around garden courtyards and an on-site restaurant using organic produce grown on the property. A lower altitude base that suits clients easing into the trip gently. I can price this one up for you.

Best luxury

Belmond Rio Sagrado, Sacred Valley

Suites set along the Urubamba River with their own spa, two restaurants and a train station on site for the journey to Machu Picchu. Guests can feed the hotel's own baby alpacas, a lovely touch for families. A genuine treat it stay for anyone after real luxury. I can price this one up for you.

Best right by the ruins

Inkaterra Machu Picchu Pueblo Hotel

A lodge style hotel on the edge of Aguas Calientes, the town below the ruins, with open fireplaces, orchid gardens, a well regarded restaurant and guides who know the area inside out. The best choice for anyone who wants to be first through the gates in the morning. I can price this one up for you.

Facilities as published at the time of writing. I always check current pricing, board basis and availability before booking.

Getting around

The journey from the Sacred Valley to Machu Picchu is done by train, either from Ollantaytambo or from Poroy near Cusco, run by PeruRail or IncaRail, up through the Urubamba gorge to Aguas Calientes, then a short shuttle bus up the final climb to the citadel itself. Taxis within Cusco and around the Sacred Valley towns are plentiful and inexpensive, and I arrange private transfers for arrivals, departures and the drive between Cusco and the valley as standard, since self driving here is not something I recommend given the mountain roads and altitude.

Places to visit

Ollantaytambo

A living Inca town where people still live within the original stone walls and water channels, with a steep terraced fortress above the square. One of the best preserved Inca settlements anywhere, and the usual departure point for the train to Machu Picchu.

Rainbow Mountain

Vinicunca's naturally striped slopes, coloured by mineral deposits, have become one of Peru's most photographed sights. It is a genuinely demanding day out at over 5,000 metres, so I always check a client's fitness and acclimatisation before booking it.

Chinchero & the Sacred Valley markets

Chinchero's Sunday market and its weaving cooperatives, where the same natural dye techniques have been used for centuries, make a lovely, colourful stop on the way between Cusco and the valley, alongside the Pisac market on other days of the week.

Humantay Lake

A turquoise glacial lake at around 4,200 metres, reached by a steep but manageable hike past herds of llamas and alpacas grazing the high pasture. A full day out from Cusco and a proper taste of the high Andes without the crowds of Machu Picchu itself.

Multi centre: combining Peru with somewhere else

Peru does not come as one simple flight and hotel package, so I build it as a tailor made trip from the outset, and that makes it straightforward to extend. Here are three real ways I would put it together.

Peru & Lake Titicaca

Add two or three nights in Puno, on the shore of the highest navigable lake in the world, for a boat trip out to the Uros floating islands, built from reeds by hand for generations. About a six to seven hour overland transfer or a short flight from Cusco.

Peru & the Amazon Rainforest

Fly from Cusco to Puerto Maldonado, gateway to the Tambopata National Reserve, and finish the trip with two or three nights at Inkaterra Reserva Amazonica, a genuine jungle lodge with guided canopy walks and night time wildlife spotting by boat.

Peru & the Southern Coast

Head south from Lima instead, flying over the mysterious Nazca Lines, visiting the Ballestas Islands from Paracas for wildlife close up, and finishing in Arequipa for Colca Canyon, one of the deepest canyons on earth and home to soaring Andean condors.

These are built as separate bookings combined into one trip rather than a single packaged product, message me and I will put the whole itinerary and transfers together for you.
